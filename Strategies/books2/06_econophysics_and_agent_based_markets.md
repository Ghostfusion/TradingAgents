# Econophysics and agent-based markets: a stylised fact you measure can be an artifact of the horizon, the estimator, or the mean field

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief reads the agent-based and kinetic-exchange half of the econophysics census for a research and analysis layer, not an execution layer. The corpus slice is the arXiv q-fin.GN topic "econophysics and agent models": 148 records spanning 2000-2025, 51 of them carrying a journal reference, 16 from 2021 onward. From 40 screened candidates, 12 papers were read in depth at the PDF, and every number below carries a page marker taken from the file. The purpose is to say which statistic to add, which measurement to distrust, and which number to stop publishing. The sibling library already covers the scaling, multifractal, Hawkes and rough-volatility material in `Strategies/books/18-econophysics.md`; this brief adds the microscopic-exchange, agent-herding, limit-order-book and calibration strands and defers to that book rather than restating it.

## What this covers

The slice runs from the microscopic rule of a wealth exchange to the macroscopic distribution it produces, and then to the discipline of fitting such a model to data. It covers the exponential body and the Pareto tail of wealth and the demonstration that the tail is an overlap of exponentials and a finite-size transient; Gibrat's law as the lognormal null for firm growth; the way interaction topology and wealth-similarity preference reshape the distribution; herding models that reproduce the leverage effect and the correlation eigen-structure; the zero-intelligence limit-order book as an exact null; minority-game coordination and its critical points; and finally parameter degeneracy, non-unique continuum limits and optimization instability.

The single most consequential finding for this repository is negative: several of the summary statistics a research layer is tempted to publish as stationary facts - a wealth or return tail exponent, a leverage-effect correlation, a fitted agent parameter, a backtested window - are transients or non-identifiable quantities in the very models that generate them. The measured number is real; what fails is the claim that it is a stable property of the system.

## The short answer

1. The mean-field wealth tail is not stationary for finite N: the Bouchaud-Mezard wealth-redistribution model has no stationary wealth distribution at any finite agent count, and for N = 10^4 with sigma^2 = 0.5 the power-law regime lasts only about t in [2, 300] before synchronization (`0809.4139v2`, p.4, p.5).
2. That tail is not one law but a mixture: slicing the quenched saving propensities into ten bins of width 0.1 gives mostly exponential partial distributions whose overlap reproduces the power law (`physics/0504153v2`, p.13).
3. Fitting an agent-based market model to stylised facts does not identify its parameters: beta and gamma enter one product in the extended Chiarella demand, so both cannot be estimated and beta is fixed at 10 (`2208.14207v1`, p.10).
4. A discretised agent model's continuum limit is not unique, and the naive explicit scheme blows up where the continuous system provably does not (`1904.04951v3`, p.2, p.13, p.21).
5. A single investor who re-optimises a backtested parameter never converges: the parameter cycles and does not settle even though every other trader is static, and the market price variation is enlarged (`2202.00831v1`, p.4, p.5).
6. Trade-acceptance rules that are symmetric in the two partners do not change the equilibrium distribution at all; only the relaxation time moves (`1505.01274v1`, p.6).
7. The zero-intelligence limit-order book has closed-form scalings that make it an exact null - spread s = (mu + v)/lambda and diffusion D = 2 (mu + v)^3 / lambda^2 - and the published diffusion law for the market-order-dominated case was wrong (`2510.01814v1`, p.18, p.20, p.21).
8. Herding alone generates the leverage effect with an exponential decay of about 19 days, and symmetrising the herding asymmetry removes it (`1407.5258v1`, p.6, p.7).
9. Interaction structure, not the exchange rule, sets the distribution: on a network the stationary mean wealth is proportional to degree, and a preference for trading with similar wealth splits the population into classes with no steady state (`0809.4139v2`, p.7; `2305.16238v1`, p.9).
10. Coordination has critical points: minority-game predictability vanishes below alpha_c = 0.3374, and a fully crowd-avoiding rule serves fewer customers (utilization about 0.46) than random choice (about 0.63) (`1305.2121v3`, p.10, p.18).
11. Multi-level herding reproduces the first three eigenvalues of a cross-correlation matrix only qualitatively - NYSE (26.01, 7.45, 5.13) against a simulated (24.62, 7.93, 3.82) - with no formal goodness-of-fit test (`1504.01811v1`, p.13).
12. Gibrat's law gives an explicit lognormal null for firm size with variance e^(2t) - 1 and moment growth rate n(n - 1), so a heavier tail may be claimed only after the lognormal fails (`1606.04796v1`, p.9).

## 1. Wealth distributions: an exponential body and a fragile Pareto tail

The canonical kinetic exchange model gives N agents wealths, picks two at random each step, and conserves the total exchanged (`physics/0504153v2`, p.3). With no saving the dynamics converges to an exponential (Boltzmann) distribution; adding a global saving propensity 0 < lambda < 1 makes the equilibrium a Gamma density with a positive mode, whose effective dimension is D_lambda/2 = (1 + 2 lambda)/(1 - lambda) and whose effective temperature is T_lambda = [(1 - lambda)/(1 + 2 lambda)] times the mean wealth, reducing to the Boltzmann law at D_lambda = 2 (`physics/0504153v2`, p.5). Quenched, heterogeneous saving propensities instead produce a power-law tail with Pareto exponent alpha = 1 (`physics/0504153v2`, p.8), against a background estimate that real Pareto exponents lie between 1 and 2 (`physics/0504153v2`, p.3). The mechanism of that tail is the part worth carrying: sliced into partial distributions, each saving bin is essentially exponential, and the sum or overlap of these exponentials reproduces the power law (`physics/0504153v2`, p.13).

The immediate-exchange model reaches the same family from a different rule. Two agents swap fractions eps_j x_j and eps_k x_k drawn uniformly, and the equilibrium is a Gamma density with alpha = beta = 2, that is f_2(x) = 4 x exp(-2 x) with mode 1/2 and f_2(0) = 0 (`1505.01274v1`, p.3). That is exactly the equilibrium of the Chakraborti-Chakrabarti saving model at saving propensity lambda = 1/4 (`1505.01274v1`, p.4), and the fitted shape alpha around 2 sits inside the range (2, 2.5) reported for household income distributions (`1505.01274v1`, p.3). The point for a research layer is that these distributions are generated by the exchange rule, not fitted to markets; they are hypotheses about mechanism, and the mechanism is thin.

`0809.4139v2` turns the fragility into a breakdown of the mean-field approximation itself. In the Bouchaud-Mezard wealth-redistribution model with multiplicative noise of strength sigma, the mean-field stationary law has tail exponent 2 + 1/sigma^2, so a realistic exponent near 2 requires sigma approximately 1 (`0809.4139v2`, p.3). The exact moment equations say otherwise. The free regime ends at t1 = 1/(2 (1 - sigma^2)) (`0809.4139v2`, p.3), and the exact second-moment equations have only the trivial stationary solution, so for any finite N there is no stationary distribution (`0809.4139v2`, p.4). Correlations then grow to the synchronized regime at t2 = (1 - sigma^2) N and t3 = (1 - sigma^2) N/(2 sigma^2) (`0809.4139v2`, p.4, p.5). For 10^4 agents the power-law window is roughly t in [2, 300] and by t greater than 10^4 the system is synchronized, with the population-based variance and the ensemble-based variance no longer interchangeable (`0809.4139v2`, p.5, p.6). A wealth tail read off a finite simulation is therefore a transient of an unresolved relaxation, not a property to publish as a constant.

## 2. Gibrat's law and the lognormal null for growth

`1606.04796v1` derives the continuous limit of Gibrat's law of proportionate effect, in which a firm's size changes by a random amount proportional to its current size (`1606.04796v1`, p.2). The limit is a linear diffusion equation whose source-type solution is exactly a lognormal density (`1606.04796v1`, p.9). Two numbers matter. The variance of that lognormal grows as e^(2t) - 1 and vanishes as t goes to 0, and the moments of order n greater than or equal to 2 grow at rate n(n - 1), so the distribution spreads without bound under pure Gibrat dynamics (`1606.04796v1`, p.9). The convergence of general initial data to the source-type solution is bounded by C/sqrt(1 + 2t), a sharp rate (`1606.04796v1`, p.9, p.10). A different small-perturbation limit gives the opposite end state: a first-order model whose solution collapses toward a point mass, so the same microscopic rule supports both spreading and concentration depending on the scaling chosen (`1606.04796v1`, p.5). For a research layer this is the clean null against which a "heavier than lognormal" claim about sizes, returns or growth should be tested before any power law is asserted.

## 3. Who trades with whom: topology and preference set the shape

Two papers say that who trades with whom matters more than how the trade is priced. On a general exchange network, the stationary mean wealth of agent i is proportional to its degree, <v_i> = k_i/z with z the average degree, so a power-law wealth distribution can arise purely as a topological effect of a scale-free degree distribution (`0809.4139v2`, p.7). For networks of small average degree the power-law regime is brief or absent: the transition times t2' and t3' scale as O(z) rather than as N (`0809.4139v2`, p.8, p.9). The authors directly contradict Bouchaud and Mezard's earlier numerical claim of stationary power-law tails at z = 4, attributing it to finite-size and finite-time effects (`0809.4139v2`, p.9).

`2305.16238v1` adds preference. In the Nearest Neighbour Wealth Model the second agent is drawn from those whose wealth is just above or below the first, over a population of 256 agents averaged over 1000 realisations; the result is a quasi-oligarchic society in which a large fraction of wealth circulates among very few agents, but never concentrates in one, and the interaction never stops (`2305.16238v1`, p.4, p.5). No stable overall distribution emerges, only a stable distribution for the top holder, and enlarging the neighbour bound moves the shape continuously toward the exponential with no phase transition (`2305.16238v1`, p.5). In the Delta Distance Wealth Model, interactions occur only when the pre-trade wealth gap is below a threshold delta; with 10^4 agents and 10^6 interactions the population segregates into a poor and a rich class with different means, and the segregation does not attain a steady state (`2305.16238v1`, p.8, p.9). For delta = 10 about 10 percent of agents hold about 40 percent of the wealth; for delta = 5 at least 10 percent hold at least 70 percent (`2305.16238v1`, p.9). The distribution is independent of the agent count but changes with the average wealth (`2305.16238v1`, p.10). The authors label the work a pilot study. The reading for a research layer is that a persistent class split in a cross-section can be a crowding artifact of who trades with whom rather than an equilibrium fact.

## 4. Non-identifiability of a kinetic model from its equilibrium

`1505.01274v1` introduces a probabilistic trading criterion: each unit accepts a trade with a probability that depends on the profit or loss of that trade (`1505.01274v1`, p.5). With a symmetric criterion, the equilibrium wealth distribution is the same Gamma f_2(x) with shape alpha = 2 for every value of the criterion parameter, and the same holds for the exponential acceptance form across its shift values; only the relaxation process to equilibrium changes, so a smaller tolerance corresponds to a longer relaxation time (`1505.01274v1`, p.6). The authors state the conclusion plainly: introducing a decision process that could be read as intelligence in the units is irrelevant for the final state, as long as the acceptance criterion is symmetric in the two trading partners (`1505.01274v1`, p.6). Asymmetric criteria do change the shape, and sufficiently strong pro-rich asymmetry makes the small-wealth density diverge (`1505.01274v1`, p.6, p.7). Mixing immediate and unidirectional interactions gives alpha(mu) = 2^(1 - 2 mu), so half unidirectional brings the exponential (`1505.01274v1`, p.5). The lesson is a non-identifiability result: a matched equilibrium does not certify that a decision rule was captured, and any calibration that fits only the distribution is fitting a one-parameter family that many rules share.

## 5. Herding: the leverage effect and the correlation eigen-structure

`1407.5258v1` builds a daily agent model with N = 10000 agents whose trading probability and herding degree both depend asymmetrically on the previous return (`1407.5258v1`, p.3, p.4). The two key parameters are read from market data rather than fitted to the simulation: the volume ratio V+/V- gives alpha = 1.01 for the S&P 500 and 1.09 for Shanghai from ratios of 1.03 and 1.21, and the herding asymmetry maps linearly as delta_R = 38.2 delta_r, giving delta_R = 3 and 2 (`1407.5258v1`, p.5, p.6). Only the Shanghai value of alpha is statistically distinguishable from 1 (p = 8.4 x 10^-4) (`1407.5258v1`, p.5). The model then reproduces the leverage effect for five indices and the anti-leverage effect for Shanghai, with an exponential fit whose decay time is about 19 days for the leverage effect and 8 days for the anti-leverage effect (`1407.5258v1`, p.6). The causal check is an ablation: replacing the asymmetric herding degree with a symmetric one makes the leverage effect disappear and weakens the anti-leverage effect, while for the five leverage markets the trading asymmetry is nearly symmetric and contributes little (`1407.5258v1`, p.7). The investment-horizon maximum M = 150 and the base trading probability p = 0.0154 are inputs, and 20000 points are produced with the first 10000 discarded for equilibration (`1407.5258v1`, p.6, p.7).

`1504.01811v1` extends the same idea to three levels - herding within a stock, within a sector, and across the market - with 150 stocks in 5 sectors and N = 600000 agents (`1504.01811v1`, p.5). Calibrating the co-movement parameters on NYSE stocks from 1990-2006 and HKSE stocks from 2003-2011 gives H_M = 0.363 for NYSE and 0.306 for HKSE, with sector values between 0.34 and 0.55 (`1504.01811v1`, p.14). The headline claim is that the three largest correlation-matrix eigenvalues match between the real and simulated markets: NYSE (26.01, 7.45, 5.13) against simulated (24.62, 7.93, 3.82), HKSE (21.70, 3.06, 1.89) against (21.43, 4.57, 2.97) (`1504.01811v1`, p.13). The market-mode eigenvector is nearly uniform and the next two are dominated by a single sector, reproduced in simulation (`1504.01811v1`, p.5). The group buying probability is P = 0.363 for NYSE and 0.317 for HKSE, and results are reported as robust to the agent number (450000 to 750000) and the horizon length (800 to 1200 days) (`1504.01811v1`, p.5, p.7). Unlike its companion, this paper reports no formal goodness-of-fit statistic, so the agreement is qualitative. The relevant caution is that a correlation matrix with a dominant market mode and a few sector modes can be generated by herding alone, which is exactly the structure a random-matrix null is meant to separate from real correlation.

## 6. The zero-intelligence order book: an exact null, and where its mean field fails

`2510.01814v1` revisits the Santa Fe zero-intelligence limit-order book, in which limit submissions arrive as a Poisson process of intensity lambda times the tick, cancellations at intensity v, and unit market orders at intensity mu (`2510.01814v1`, p.3, p.4). The contribution is a systematic derivation: an exact master equation, an exact first-order BBGKY hierarchy and a mean-field Boltzmann equation, with the boundary condition rho_st(0) = lambda/(v + mu) derived exactly in the small-tick-size limit and without the mean-field approximation (`2510.01814v1`, p.18). The resulting closed scalings are the useful part. The average spread is s = (mu + v)/lambda, the instantaneous price impact is (mu + v)/(2 lambda), and the diffusion constant is D = 2 (mu + v)^3 / lambda^2 (`2510.01814v1`, p.20, p.21). When cancellations dominate (mu much less than v) this gives s = v/lambda, D = 2 v^3/lambda^2 and impact v/(2 lambda); when market orders dominate (mu much greater than v) it gives s = mu/lambda, D = 2 mu^3/lambda^2 and impact mu/(2 lambda) (`2510.01814v1`, p.20, p.21).

Two corrections matter. First, the earlier published dimensional analysis claimed D proportional to v mu^2/lambda^2 for the market-order-dominated case, and the authors trace that to a misspecified characteristic constant and show the correct non-dimensionalisation yields D proportional to mu^3/lambda^2 (`2510.01814v1`, p.21, p.22). Second, the mean-field theory is only partially valid for large mu: the numerical diffusion constant behaves as approximately (mu v)^(3/2)/lambda^2 while the mean field predicts mu^3/lambda^2, and the authors attribute the gap to non-Markovian mean reversion in which the permanent price impact decouples from the instantaneous one (`2510.01814v1`, p.22, p.23). For a research layer the null is the point: a synthetic or vendor spread-and-impact model can be validated against s = v/lambda and D = 2 v^3/lambda^2 before any behavioural agents are added, and no permanent-equals-instantaneous impact assumption should be carried into a market-order-heavy read.

## 7. Coordination and the minority mechanism: critical points and long transients

`1305.2121v3` reviews statistical-mechanics models of competitive resource allocation: N agents choosing among R resources (`1305.2121v3`, p.3). The minority game has control parameter alpha = P/N, where P is the number of strategies per agent, and its scaled fluctuations sigma^2/N have a clear minimum near alpha = 0.4 whose precise location depends on N (`1305.2121v3`, p.7, p.10). The analytic critical point from the replica calculation is alpha_c = 0.3374, where the response function diverges and the predictability H vanishes, with H/N behaving as (alpha - alpha_c)^2 near the transition (`1305.2121v3`, p.10). The signal-to-noise crossover is quantified by the constant K approximately 0.39 and the region of anomalous fluctuations scales as N^(-1/4) (`1305.2121v3`, p.8, p.9). For a simpler P = 1 learning model the critical learning rate is gamma_c = 2, above which the population herds and fluctuations scale as N^2 (`1305.2121v3`, p.5, p.13). Convergence to the stationary state is expected to scale as N/alpha above the critical point, a long transient that numerical work must burn in (`1305.2121v3`, p.8).

The Kolkata Paise Restaurant problem gives the coordination counterpart with an exact benchmark. With random choice the average fraction of restaurants occupied is f = 1 - exp(-g) for customer-to-restaurant ratio g, giving f about 0.63 at g = 1 (`1305.2121v3`, p.18). Rank-dependent strategies give f about 0.57 when the exponent equals 2 (`1305.2121v3`, p.18). A fully crowd-avoiding rule gives f about 0.46, worse than random choice and typical of herding, while a stochastic crowd-avoiding rule with return probability 1/n serves about 0.79 with a log N convergence time (`1305.2121v3`, p.18). The review also reports that any model in which agents decide to trade or not on the sign of a random walk acquires long memory in its activity, and by extension in volatility (`1305.2121v3`, p.15). The operational translation is twofold: agent experiments need an explicit burn-in, and a naive crowding-avoidance rule can underperform a random allocation.

## 8. Calibration discipline: degeneracy, surrogate and a non-unique continuum

`2208.14207v1` calibrates the extended Chiarella model, reduced to three agents - one fundamental, one momentum, one noise - against 75 stocks on Nasdaq, the London Stock Exchange and the Hong Kong exchange, on minute data from 20 September to 30 November 2021 (`2208.14207v1`, p.6). It fits alpha, gamma and sigma_N while fixing rho = 0.1 and beta = 10, and minimises a stylised-facts distance D = KS + V + ACF1 + ACF2 with all four weights set to 1 (`2208.14207v1`, p.9, p.10). The paper states its own degeneracy clearly: beta and gamma appear only in the product gamma tanh(beta M_t), so estimating both is unreliable and fixing beta is what makes the calibration robust (`2208.14207v1`, p.10). The optimiser is an XGBoost surrogate trained on a Sobol pool of 16384 parameter combinations starting from 2000 evaluated points, and the surrogate distance prediction error is under 5 percent at the predicted optimum (`2208.14207v1`, p.11, p.12). The surrogate beats expectation-maximisation on average by roughly 10 percent in distance and wins on 22 of 25 Nasdaq names and all 25 Hong Kong names (`2208.14207v1`, p.12). The authors show one agent per category is sufficient at minute frequency.

`1904.04951v3` attacks the same problem at the mathematical level for the Levy-Levy-Solomon and Franke-Westerhoff models (`1904.04951v3`, p.1). Its central caveat is that the continuum limit is not uniquely defined when one starts from the difference equations the literature actually writes down (`1904.04951v3`, p.2, p.9). The time-scaling choice changes conclusions: roughly 90 percent of optimal investment decisions sit on the boundary of the interval [0.01, 0.99] in the original model; with the memory variable scaled by the time step the share falls to 72 percent at dt = 0.1 and all decisions become interior at dt = 0.01, while with fixed memory it stays near 90 percent for any dt and prices oscillate (`1904.04951v3`, p.10). The naive explicit Euler discretisation of the time-continuous Franke-Westerhoff system blows up at a fundamentalist noise of 1.15 even at dt = 0.1, because the agent fractions leave [0, 1], whereas the continuous system is provably bounded; a semi-implicit discretisation preserves that stability at comparable cost (`1904.04951v3`, p.13, p.15, p.21). The authors recommend modelling at the continuous level precisely so the time scale and the discretisation are explicit choices rather than hidden ones.

## 9. Adaptation and optimization instability

`2202.00831v1` is the most directly operational paper in the slice. One stock trades in a continuous double auction with 1000 normal agents plus a momentum technical agent and a reversal technical agent, with fundamental value 10000, a tick of 0.01, an order size of 100 and a run of 20 million ticks (`2202.00831v1`, p.3, p.4). Each technical agent's parameter is chosen by particle-swarm optimisation that backtests over the whole simulation run, with 200 particles and 50 iterations over the window [100, 300000], and the random numbers are fixed so an unchanged parameter reproduces the run exactly (`2202.00831v1`, p.4). The result is that the momentum parameter never converges to a stable value; it cycles (case 3 of the authors' taxonomy), and with both technical agents present the parameter changes irregularly (`2202.00831v1`, p.5). Prices are destabilised relative to the no-technical-agent case, and the paper's headline conclusion is that a single investor who optimises by backtesting destabilises a market in which everyone else is fixed, because backtesting cannot price the investor's own market impact (`2202.00831v1`, p.1, p.4). The consequence for a research layer is that any reported optimum is conditional on a price path that the strategy itself would have changed, so a re-optimisation stability check is the honest deliverable, not the optimum.

## Caveats

Five of the twelve papers contain no market data at all: `physics/0504153v2`, `0809.4139v2`, `1505.01274v1`, `1606.04796v1` and `2305.16238v1` are Monte Carlo or analytic exercises that cite real-data numbers only as background (for example the Pareto exponent between 1 and 2 at `physics/0504153v2` p.3, and sigma near 1 at `0809.4139v2` p.3). `1305.2121v3` is a review with no single dataset, `1904.04951v3` performs no statistical calibration at all (p.9, p.12), and `2510.01814v1` is a theory paper validated against its own simulation (p.22). The herding results are single-family and single-period: `1407.5258v1` uses six indices over unequal windows spanning 1950-2012, with only the Shanghai alpha significant (`1407.5258v1`, p.4, p.5), and `1504.01811v1` reduces every investor to a holder of exactly one stock (`1504.01811v1`, p.3) and reports its eigenvalue agreement with no formal goodness-of-fit test. `2208.14207v1` covers one 72-day intraday window in 2021 and three exchanges only. `2202.00831v1` is one stock, one parameter and two technical agents, and it is a mechanism demonstration, not a forecast. `2305.16238v1` is explicitly a pilot study at N = 256. None of the models carries transaction costs, and several compare only stylised facts rather than predictive performance. This brief deliberately does not repeat the scaling, multifractal, Hawkes or rough-volatility material already covered by `Strategies/books/18-econophysics.md`, and it does not claim that any of the ABM results here constitute tradeable signal; they are diagnostics about which measured regularity is stable and which is an artifact.

## Papers read in depth

- `0809.4139v2` - Breakdown of the mean-field approximation in a wealth distribution model (2008). Shows the stationary wealth law is not stationary for finite N and gives the transient times; a model result, not an observation.
- `physics/0504153v2` - Kinetic theory models for the distribution of wealth: power law from overlap of exponentials (2005). Explains the Pareto tail as an overlap of Gamma partial distributions; simulation-only with no market fit.
- `1505.01274v1` - Kinetic models of immediate exchange (2015). Immediate exchange gives a Gamma equilibrium invariant to symmetric acceptance rules; a non-identifiability result, not an empirical one.
- `1606.04796v1` - Kinetic and mean field description of Gibrat's law (2016). Rigorous diffusion limit and lognormal source-type solution for firm size, with variance e^(2t) - 1; no data.
- `2305.16238v1` - Role of Neighbouring Wealth Preference in Kinetic Exchange model of market (2023). A wealth-similarity preference creates classes and destroys the steady state; the authors call it a pilot study.
- `1407.5258v1` - Agent-based model with asymmetric trading and herding for complex financial systems (2014). Calibrates parameters from market data and reproduces leverage and anti-leverage with tau about 19 and 8 days; six indices over uneven windows.
- `1504.01811v1` - Agent-based model with multi-level herding for complex financial systems (2015). Three-level herding reproduces the correlation eigen-structure on NYSE and HKSE; no formal goodness-of-fit.
- `2510.01814v1` - Mean-field theory of the Santa Fe model revisited (2025). Closed zero-intelligence-book scalings and an exact boundary condition, correcting an earlier diffusion law; the mean field fails for market-order-dominated regimes.
- `1305.2121v3` - Statistical Mechanics of Competitive Resource Allocation using Agent-based Models (2013). Physics Reports review of minority games and crowd-avoiding queues with the critical point alpha_c = 0.3374; no empirical dataset.
- `2208.14207v1` - Calibrating the extended Chiarella model (2022). Stylised-facts distance with an XGBoost surrogate across 75 stocks; beta/gamma degeneracy and only three agents.
- `1904.04951v3` - Robust Mathematical Formulation and Probabilistic Description of Agent-Based Computational Economic Market Models (2019). Proves the continuum limit is non-unique and the naive discretisation blows up; no statistical calibration.
- `2202.00831v1` - Instability of financial markets by optimizing investment strategies investigated by an agent-based model (2022). One optimising investor destabilises a static market; one stock, one parameter, not a forecast.
