# Market design, regulation and fees: a rule change is a dated change to a decision contract, and a fee is a policy the owner sets

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief reads the regulation and market-structure half of the q-fin.GN harvest for a research and analysis layer, not an execution engine: what regulatory interventions, disclosure regimes and venue design rules do to prices, volumes, depth and the location of trading. Its slice is 43 records matching regulation and market-design policy between 2007 and 2026, 11 of which carry a journal reference; 40 candidates were screened and 10 were read at their local PDFs. It is written for the operators of this repository, whose research decisions are gated by `enable_*` config keys, validated against `contracts/research_decision.v1.schema.json`, and scored by the score engines. The organising idea is that a rule change is exactly a change to a decision contract - to what may be traded, priced, held or charged - and that both a fee and a rule are values the owner sets, so the research layer should carry them as dated, switchable, and measured rather than assumed.

## What this covers

The slice arcs from single enforcement events (SEC security classifications), through regime switches (the July 2017 DAO Report, ChiNext's approval-to-registration reform, MiCA's stablecoin authorisation), to the design margins where a rule bites hardest: venue opacity, settlement horizons, margin thresholds, priority fees and mandated risk measures. Its single most consequential finding for this repository is that rules relocate and reshape activity rather than destroy it, which makes a rule event a change in the weights and the available menu, not a single knock-out shock. After the DAO Report Europe gains about 14 additional ICOs per region-month relative to other regions while global issuance keeps running (`2602.00138v1`, p.17); under MiCA the aggregate stablecoin share barely moves, from 17.70 to 18.24 percent, while the exchange cross-section shifts 0.82 pre-event standard deviations toward USDC (`2607.09514v1`, pp.2, 9).

The second finding is that the design lever is often larger than the enforcement lever. Lengthening a prediction contract from five to fifteen minutes removes the settlement-manipulation signature that a series of enforcement actions had not (`2606.31675v1`, pp.6-8); a listing-day cap does not remove IPO overreaction but stretches it and lifts the measured first return from 34.08 to 334.60 percent (`2212.11779v1`, pp.22-23). Read together with the repo's own gating pattern, where every rule is a `enable_*` switch shipped off by default (docs/gate_registry.md), this says the correct research move is to make the rule and the fee an explicit, dated, owner-set parameter of the calculation, and to measure what happens when it is on.

## The short answer

1. Enforcement reprices: SEC security classifications cost named crypto assets 17.2 percent cumulative abnormal return over 30 days, deepening from -2.4 percent pre-announcement (`2412.02452v1`, p.15).
2. The same events drain volume: cumulative abnormal volume is -4.952 log units over 30 days for the full sample (`2412.02452v1`, p.16).
3. The damage concentrates in volatile, illiquid names: volatility beta -0.765 on day 0 deepening to -5.151 at 30 days, illiquidity -1.622 at [0,2] (`2412.02452v1`, p.8).
4. Volume drains before the announcement while price does not move: volatility -8.534 and illiquidity -0.410 in the week before, an informed-trading signature (`2412.02452v1`, p.9).
5. A delisting mandate reallocates the venue cross-section while aggregates barely move: DiD 0.818 pre-event standard deviations, aggregate USDC share 17.70 to 18.24 percent (`2607.09514v1`, pp.2, 9).
6. The shift is a contraction on the delisted leg, not growth in the substitute: USDT volume falls about 20 percent where it is delisted while USDC volume is flat (`2607.09514v1`, p.17).
7. Enforcement reallocates issuance across borders: Europe gains 13.88 additional ICOs per region-month after the DAO Report against a pre-treatment mean of 2.60 (`2602.00138v1`, p.17).
8. A listing-regime change rewrites what prices on: under ChiNext registration the initial return is explained entirely by pre-listing issuer value (adjusted R-squared 0.082) (`2212.11779v1`, p.25).
9. Listing-day caps do not remove overreaction, they stretch it: mean initial return moves 34.08 percent to 334.60 percent to 190.60 percent across the three regimes (`2212.11779v1`, pp.22-23, 25).
10. Better disclosure moves investor behaviour: the disposition effect is 0.0138 smaller (average marginal effect) for the highest-transparency group, 0.0174 against 0.0312 (`2605.07352v1`, p.9).
11. Venue opacity has no uniform sign: dark pools improve price discovery when signals are precise and impair it when they are noisy (`1612.08486v1`, p.1).
12. Contract horizon, not enforcement, removed settlement manipulation: the effect is sharp at five minutes, attenuated at fifteen, and 93 percent of the $8.2 million loss falls on retail (`2606.31675v1`, pp.6-8).

## 1. Rule changes reroute flow; they rarely reduce it

The DAO Report of July 2017 applied US securities law to many ICOs without changing any statute (`2602.00138v1`, p.2). In a balanced region-month panel of eight regions over 96 months, Europe's differential post-2017 ICO count is 13.88 per region-month against a pre-treatment European mean of 2.60, month-clustered standard error 6.63, R-squared 0.847 over 768 observations (`2602.00138v1`, p.17). The raw counts move from 109 European ICOs against 145 elsewhere before the report to 1,008 against 1,014 after, so the level rose everywhere and Europe rose faster (`2602.00138v1`, p.15). A placebo that reassigns treatment to each control region gives Europe about 13.9 against Asia about 4.4 and North America about 1.3 (`2602.00138v1`, p.19). Excluding the major European crypto hubs (Switzerland, UK, Estonia, Malta, Cyprus, Gibraltar) cuts the coefficient to 5.66 but preserves significance, so the reallocation was broad-based (`2602.00138v1`, p.21). A log-linear specification with Driscoll-Kraay errors reports 0.887 on log(1 + count) (`2602.00138v1`, p.21).

MiCA shows the same pattern on a two-month venue event window. USDC's aggregate share of USDT-USDC volume moves only from 17.70 to 18.24 percent over the 15 days either side of 1 April 2025, and the aggregate USDC/USDT volume ratio from 0.215 to 0.223, because the aggregate is dominated by venues that kept listing USDT (`2607.09514v1`, pp.2, 5). In the cross-section of 14 exchanges (four regulated-facing, ten global), the smoothed USDC share falls 0.243 pre-event standard deviations on global venues and rises 0.576 on regulated-facing ones, a difference-in-differences of 0.818 (exchange-clustered p = 0.004) or about six percentage points raw, from pre-event levels near 7 and 46 percent (`2607.09514v1`, p.9). Exact randomization inference over all 1001 treatment assignments confirms it (two-sided p = 0.006), as does a Rademacher wild cluster bootstrap (`2607.09514v1`, p.19). The practical lesson is that a rule event is a change to the venue-level asset menu and its relative weights, and its effect is invisible in the aggregate that most summaries report.

## 2. What an enforcement event does to prices and depth

The SEC's classifications of specific tokens as securities are the cleanest enforcement shock in the slice: 48 event dates covering 117 asset-level classifications, with a market model estimated over [-150, -10] days and Bitcoin as benchmark (`2412.02452v1`, pp.4, 6). Cumulative abnormal returns across all events deepen from -0.024 before the event, through -0.052 at [0,2], -0.122 at [0,6] and -0.135 at [0,13], to -0.172 at [0,30] (`2412.02452v1`, p.15). The two-day Binance and Coinbase cluster is worse: -0.065 at [0,2] and -0.226 at [0,13] after a positive pre-announcement 0.025, which the authors read as a momentum reversal (`2412.02452v1`, p.15). The Bittrex enforcement sub-sample reaches -0.252 at [0,30], while the Coinbase insider-trading window shows no significant post-announcement CAR but a -0.039 abnormal return on day -1 (`2412.02452v1`, p.15).

Liquidity moves with it, in the same direction. Cumulative abnormal volume over 30 days is -4.952 log units for the full sample, -10.848 for the Coinbase insider-trading window, and positive only in the Bittrex window, which peaks at 3.474 at [0,13] (`2412.02452v1`, p.16). Cross-sectional regressions put the day-0 effect on more volatile assets at -0.765, deepening to -5.151 over 30 days, and the [0,2] effect on more illiquid assets at -1.622, with larger market capitalisation also negative at -0.007 (`2412.02452v1`, p.8). Pre-announcement volume coefficients are significant where price coefficients are not: volatility -8.534 and illiquidity -0.410 in the week before the announcement (`2412.02452v1`, p.9). The authors are explicit that this volume-first pattern is consistent with, but does not prove, informed trading, because identification is at the event level rather than the trade level (`2412.02452v1`, p.9). For a research layer the usable part is the conditioning cross-section: the stress is worst in names that are already volatile and illiquid, which are measurable before the event.

## 3. Regime changes rewrite what prices on, not just how much

ChiNext gives a three-period natural experiment on listing regimes. Before the 2013 reform the board ran an approval regime with intraday listing-day curbs; from 2014 it kept approval but capped the listing day at 44 percent up and 36 percent down with plus or minus 10 percent daily limits thereafter; from August 2020 it moved to registration with no cap for the first five trading days and a plus or minus 20 percent daily limit (`2212.11779v1`, pp.3, 8-9). The IPO pipeline was suspended for 438 days to implement the 2013 round (`2212.11779v1`, p.8). Mean initial return goes from 34.08 percent in period 1 (`2212.11779v1`, p.22), to 334.60 percent in the capped period (`2212.11779v1`, p.23), to 190.60 percent under registration (`2212.11779v1`, p.25).

The composition of the drivers changes with the regime, which is the more usable finding. In period 1 the initial return is explained by pre-listing demand (incremental adjusted R-squared 0.360 for the category) plus market condition (0.130), with pre-listing issuer value contributing 0.010 (`2212.11779v1`, p.21). In the capped period the same return splits between pre-listing demand (0.140) and post-listing demand (0.107) (`2212.11779v1`, p.23). Under registration the initial return is explained entirely by pre-listing issuer value (0.082), with pre-listing demand dropped out altogether (`2212.11779v1`, p.25). In that final period the one-month return regression reaches an adjusted R-squared of 0.843, of which maximum drawdown alone contributes 0.740 with a standardised beta of 0.857, and the overreaction component is 27.33 percent of the initial return against 4.67 percent in the capped period (`2212.11779v1`, pp.17, 23, 25). The lesson for modelling: a regime label is not only a volatility state, it is a statement about which variables belong in the pricing model, and pooling across regimes averages away the effect you are trying to measure.

## 4. Disclosure regimes move investor behaviour

Corporate transparency is a rule-adjacent lever that operates on the order flow itself. Using 11,661 Xueqiu portfolios from June 2016 to May 2023, cleaned to 1,115,839 transactions from 11,323 portfolios and 14,112,512 stock-day observations (`2605.07352v1`, pp.4-5), the baseline disposition effect is an average marginal effect of 0.0226 (Probit) and 0.0220 (Logit) on sale probability when a position moves from loss to gain (`2605.07352v1`, p.7). Grouping by an annual transparency index built from earnings quality, exchange disclosure grades (A to D scored 4 to 1), analyst coverage, forecast accuracy and Big Four audit status (`2605.07352v1`, pp.5-6), the highest group has an average marginal effect of 0.0174 against 0.0312 in the lowest, a gap of 0.0138 significant at the 1 percent level in a Suest test (`2605.07352v1`, p.9). The mechanism is asymmetric: higher transparency lowers the probability of selling both winners and losers, but the effect on winners is larger, so the net disposition effect falls (`2605.07352v1`, p.11). Transparency also lengthens holding periods (coefficient 0.0886) and lowers trading frequency (-0.0005), both at the 1 percent level (`2605.07352v1`, p.11). For a backtest this is a warning that turnover, and therefore turnover cost, is not a stable behavioural parameter; it co-moves with the information environment.

## 5. Venue opacity helps or hurts depending on signal precision

The dark-pool result resolves an apparently contradictory empirical literature by making the effect conditional. US dark-pool share of consolidated volume grew from 7.51 percent in 2008 to 16.57 percent in 2015, with roughly 300 US equity venues including 11 exchanges and 40 active dark pools, and Australian dark liquidity at 26.2 percent of traded value (`1612.08486v1`, pp.2, 6). Execution is rationed rather than guaranteed: reported execution probabilities are 4.11 percent on NYSE-listed and 2.17 percent on Nasdaq-listed names against 31.47 and 26.48 percent for their exchange counterparts, with an older study reporting 2 to 4 percent (`1612.08486v1`, p.8). With noisy heterogeneous signals and free venue choice, informed traders sort by signal strength: strong signals to the lit exchange, moderate signals to the dark pool, weak signals to no trade (`1612.08486v1`, p.3). The resulting amplification effect is that adding a dark pool improves price discovery when information precision is high and impairs it when precision is low, which is the mirror image of older models that assumed exact information and produced a single-sign answer (`1612.08486v1`, pp.1, 5). The practical consequence is that "dark" is not a quality label; any venue contribution has to be conditioned on the precision of the signal being routed.

## 6. Thresholds, settlement and priority: the design margins

Margin rules put a hard edge in the price function. Modelling short sales under Regulation T (initial margin 50 percent) and a maintenance margin of at least 25 percent, with the short seller returning shares rather than posting cash, clearing prices are found by a fixed-point algorithm (`2102.02176v4`, p.2). There is a threshold short-interest ratio below which the clearing price is continuous in outside purchases and above which it jumps: with a linear inverse demand function and market impact b = 2/V, the threshold is S-star about 1.23/b, a short-interest ratio near 0.615, and the outside capital needed to trigger a margin call is about 6.43 percent of average daily volume (`2102.02176v4`, p.6). The case studies (initial margin 0.45, maintenance margin 0.30) put GameStop's short-interest ratio at 10.2 with an implied jump of 19.17 x p0, AMC's at 4.17 with 7.11 x p0, and a speculative Rite Aid case at 8.08 with 14.93 x p0 (`2102.02176v4`, pp.6-7). These are comparative statics with hand-picked cases, not estimated effects, but the structural point holds: short-interest is a state variable with a discontinuity in it, and a research layer that only reports the level misses the regime.

Prediction markets show that the design lever can be larger than the enforcement lever. Polymarket's five-minute Bitcoin up/down contract resolves against a Chainlink oracle that averages major spot venues, and the Binance mid sits about 2.5 basis points from it, finishing on the same side of the strike about 85 percent of the time (`2606.31675v1`, p.6). After the contract's 12 February 2026 launch, Binance spot order flow in the final ten seconds of each five-minute cycle runs about 50 percent above its pre-launch level, and about 3.9 times higher in the roughly 6 percent of cycles priced near even (`2606.31675v1`, p.6). Within ten seconds the move reverts by about a quarter in near-even cycles and a tenth elsewhere, the signature of a push rather than news (`2606.31675v1`, p.6). In cycles where the contract was priced near even, a push flipped the winner 65 percent of the time against 41 percent in ordinary trading, and even when the market gave one side a 90-to-100 percent chance a push reversed it 34 percent of the time against 1 percent (`2606.31675v1`, p.7). Identified manipulators number 821 of about 243,000 traders and take $8.2 million, 93 percent of the loss falling on retail (`2606.31675v1`, p.7). The remedy the authors test is horizon: the effect is sharp at five minutes and much attenuated at fifteen (`2606.31675v1`, p.7). For any venue that settles on a price the researcher's own trading could move, the settlement rule belongs in the decision, not in the background.

Paid priority on-chain is a fee design that changes who trades, not merely what it costs. With discrete blocks, a single clearing round per block and paid-priority ordering, informed traders bid for queue position in a priority gas auction and only traders above a valuation cutoff participate; the cutoff is strictly increasing in the number of competing informed traders and converges to the top of the valuation support as competition grows (`2605.17425v1`, pp.20, 4). Equilibrium priority fees are increasing in order size and in the number of informed traders and decreasing in pool depth (`2605.17425v1`, p.18). The consequence reverses the traditional result that competition speeds up price discovery: with paid priority, more informed traders means more truncated information and worse price discovery, and a long-enough block time can shut the market down (`2605.17425v1`, pp.4-5). The supporting pattern is that transaction sizes on the busiest Ethereum DEX averaged about $70,000 against about $1,200 for the same pair on Binance, consistent with only high-valuation traders participating (`2605.17425v1`, p.22).

## 7. Mandated risk measures concentrate the risk they measure

The sharpest structural result in the slice is that any objective, published risk rule that makes some assets cheaper to hold in regulatory capital terms will be gamed toward estimation noise, because a few securities always show a sample standard deviation far below their true one. With 1,000 securities and 60 monthly observations, about ten should show a sample standard deviation under 80 percent of the truth (`1004.1670v4`, p.8). Under the standard Basel calibration, the minimum market-risk capital is 22 times a portfolio's daily standard deviation (3.16 x 2.33 x 3) (`1004.1670v4`, p.5). The empirical test sorts CRSP stocks on their past five-year standard deviation and measures the next five years: the future-to-past standard-deviation ratios for the five quantile groups average 1.85, 1.17, 1.00, 0.81 and 0.56, so the lowest past-volatility group experiences an 85 percent higher standard deviation subsequently (`1004.1670v4`, p.14). The proposed fixes do not fully work: using daily rather than monthly observations over five years lifts n from 60 to about 1,260 and shrinks the tail bias to 0.93 of the true standard deviation, but realistic fat tails (S&P 500 daily kurtosis near 25.8) reintroduce securities that look far safer than they are, and Basel II's added stress term does not remove them (`1004.1670v4`, pp.15-17). The transferable rule for the research layer is that a regulatorily or conventionally favoured low-volatility exposure is not automatically conservative; if a rule rewards an estimate, the estimate's noise is where the crowding goes.

## 8. Costs and rules are policies the owner sets, not laws

The repository encodes a market rule as a switch with a default, and the fee surfaces are explicit policy parameters rather than constants baked in. `net_returns` subtracts a per-trade cost with `cost_bps` defaulting to 10.0 and, when an Amihud illiquidity is supplied, adds `illiq x illiq_cost_mult / 10000` (`tradingagents/strategies/evaluate.py`). `turnover_cost` charges `sum |w_t - w_{t-1}| x c_i` with a `base_cost` default of 0.001 per one-way trade and optional per-name costs (`tradingagents/strategies/evaluate.py`). `make_cost_fn` returns a flat fixed fee or, when both maker and taker rates are supplied, a maker/taker schedule, with `fixed_fee` and `maker_taker_fee` floored at zero (`tradingagents/strategies/backtest_models.py`). The tradability rules are likewise switches: `backtest_limit_threshold` defaults to 0.0, meaning the limit-up/down gate is off unless the owner sets it, and `scripts/backtest_strategy.py` reads it into `market_tradability.limit_gate`; `volume_share_vol_limit` is declared at 0.1 as a slippage participation cap but is currently read by nothing (docs/gate_registry.md). These are the places where "the fee" and "the rule" are policy, and the brief's rule is to print the value used beside every cost-adjusted statistic instead of assuming one.

The same logic governs the decision contract. `contracts/research_decision.v1.schema.json` validates the artifact the research layer emits, and its `position` and `entry_exit` members carry entry, stop, target and size as advisory fields the consumer may only lower. The downgrade-only stabilizer `stabilize_decision` in `tradingagents/strategies/decision_guardrail.py` is itself a rule set - a risk cap at Hold, a near-resistance cap, a near-support softening - and it never increases conviction away from neutral. The tradability gates in `tradingagents/strategies/market_tradability.py` (`suspended`, `limit_gate`, `volume_gate`, `deal_price_selector`) are a rulebook applied to fills, and each refusal they emit is recorded when `enable_refusal_ledger` is on (default False, `tradingagents/strategies/refusal_ledger.py`). The lesson from the papers lands here: because a rule is a decision contract, changing the rule is a code-and-config change, and the right default is to make it switchable, dated, and observable rather than silently assumed.

## Caveats

Each empirical paper is one market and one period. The SEC study is 48 events in crypto with event-level rather than trade-level identification, so its volume-first result is suggestive of informed trading, not proof (`2412.02452v1`, p.9). The MiCA study is a 30-day window on 14 exchanges with four treated units and explicitly small-sample inference, so its point estimates are the preferred basis, not asymptotic clustered errors (`2607.09514v1`, p.9). The ICO study is observational over eight regions, self-describes as unable to establish tight causal effects, and its coefficient falls from 13.88 to 5.66 when European hubs are excluded (`2602.00138v1`, pp.12, 21). The ChiNext study is one Chinese board across three non-overlapping regimes with a short post-2020 sample. The disposition study is one Chinese platform with annual transparency measures and only the latest 200 trades per portfolio, and its headline 0.0138 gap comes from a first-sale, full-liquidation specification (`2605.07352v1`, pp.9, 13). Three papers carry no market data at all: the dark-pool sorting and amplification result is theory (`1612.08486v1`), the priority-fee auction is a model with two supporting patterns (`2605.17425v1`), and the risk-measure bias pairs a theorem with a CRSP sorting exercise (`1004.1670v4`). The short-squeeze thresholds rest on three hand-picked cases with assumed margin parameters, not estimation (`2102.02176v4`). The settlement-manipulation evidence is a single contract on a single platform over about two months (`2606.31675v1`). None of these is a law of markets; each is a measured regularity in one setting, and the strength of any conclusion should not exceed the sample behind it. No record in the shortlist was marked without a local PDF, so nothing was skipped for that reason.

## Papers read in depth

- `2412.02452v1` - Uncertain Regulations, Definite Impacts: The Impact of the SEC's Regulatory Interventions on Crypto Assets (2024). 48-event study giving 17.2 percent 30-day losses, volume drains and the volatility/illiquidity conditioning; event-level identification, no trade-level proof of informed flow.
- `2607.09514v1` - Does Regulation Bite at Gateways? Evidence from MiCA and Stablecoins (2026). Difference-in-differences on 14 exchanges showing a venue-level shift to USDC with flat aggregates; 30-day window, four treated units, small-sample inference.
- `2602.00138v1` - Regulatory Migration to Europe: ICO Reallocation Following U.S. Securities Enforcement (2026). Region-month panel showing a 13.88 ICO per month shift to Europe; observational, eight regions, coefficient halves when hubs are excluded.
- `2212.11779v1` - The Impact of Regulation Regime Changes on ChiNext IPOs (2022). Three listing regimes with sharply different pricing drivers and initial returns of 34.08, 334.60 and 190.60 percent; single board, short post-2020 sample.
- `2605.07352v1` - Corporate Transparency and the Disposition Effect (2026). 14.1 million observations linking disclosure quality to a 0.0138 smaller disposition effect; annual index, single platform, last-200-trades limit.
- `1612.08486v1` - Understanding the Impacts of Dark Pools on Price Discovery (2016). Sorting and amplification effects that condition the dark-pool debate; theoretical, with no test of its own predictions.
- `2606.31675v1` - Settlement Manipulation in Prediction Markets (2026). Settlement pushes flip near-certain outcomes, 821 wallets take $8.2 million, 93 percent of the loss falls on retail; one contract, two months.
- `2102.02176v4` - Clearing prices under margin calls and the short squeeze (2021). A threshold short-interest ratio above which clearing prices jump; comparative statics with hand-picked case calibration.
- `2605.17425v1` - The Viability of Blockchain Markets under Discrete Clearing and Paid Priority (2026). Paid priority selects high valuations, truncates information and can shut a market down; model with two supporting patterns.
- `1004.1670v4` - Any Regulation of Risk Increases Risk (2010). Measurement bias in mandated risk rules, with the lowest past-volatility decile showing a 1.85 future-to-past ratio; theorem plus CRSP sorting, broad claim is an argument.
