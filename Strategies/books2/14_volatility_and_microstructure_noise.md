# Volatility, jumps and microstructure noise: pick the estimator your risk measure will be scored on, not the one that wins the forecast MSE

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief covers the volatility, jump and microstructure-noise slice of the arXiv q-fin harvest: 86 records spanning 1998-2026, 19 with a journal reference, from which 40 candidates were screened and 12 papers read in depth at the PDF. It is written for TradingAgents, a deterministic offline research and analysis layer whose volatility calculators live in `tradingagents/strategies/volatility_models.py` and whose covariance, mean-reversion, liquidity and session surfaces feed LLM analysts through wired tools. The reference extraction of the same corpus was written for an execution engine and its `crates/` and `python/nautilus_trader/` paths do not exist here; every landing site below is a research-layer surface verified on disk.

## What this covers

The slice arcs from pure forecast horse races through the economics of implied volatility to the anatomy of price jumps and the geometry of the volatility path itself. Its single most consequential finding for this repo is that the target you measure against and the loss you minimize decide the answer more than the model does. The most accurate point volatility forecaster in the set is a GARCH-GRU hybrid that cuts the S&P 500 MSE from 0.0575 to 0.0159, yet the same paper's best 5% Value-at-Risk and 1% Value-at-Risk models are plain GARCH specifications it was meant to replace, and its hybrid Expected Shortfall tests reject `2310.01063v1` (pp.13-15). A second, quieter finding runs the other way: the estimator the repo defaults to (close-to-close realized vol, with Parkinson / Garman-Klass / Yang-Zhang offered beside it) is a range-and-gap measurement, and the papers that compare range estimators to close-to-close report systematic level differences that a risk quantile consumes directly.

Two structural results then reframe the whole problem. Volatility persistence is not a constant to be estimated but a mixture of shock horizons that drifts, so a single GARCH persistence parameter is a reduced-form summary that misleads exactly when the mixture changes `2402.01354v2` (pp.20,24). And most large price moves are not news: of 37,452 jumps in 301 US stocks only about 4.3% fall within three minutes of a news item, and most are mean-reverting `2404.16467v1` (pp.3,6). A system that treats a jump as an exogenous shock, or that treats a near-unit-root persistence as structural, will misprice both.

## The short answer

1. A GARCH-GRU hybrid cuts the S&P 500 point volatility MSE from 0.0575 to 0.0159 (skewed-t GARCH) and 0.1350 to 0.0152 (skewed-t EGARCH), yet the best 5% Value-at-Risk model is a GJR-GARCH-GRU and the best 1% Value-at-Risk model is a plain APARCH, and the hybrid Expected Shortfall tests fail `2310.01063v1` (pp.13-15).
2. The hybrid's target is a scaled Garman-Klass-plus-gap (GKYZ) range estimator, so "better volatility" here means "closer to an OHLC range measurement", not "closer to the variance a risk quantile needs" `2310.01063v1` (p.7).
3. Sentiment toward macro announcements improves volatility forecasts by 14.99% on the top-10% highest-volatility days but reduces accuracy by 1.38% on the other 90% of days `2503.19767v1` (pp.28-29).
4. A graph-spectral HAR that embeds a directional volatility-spillover network beats plain HAR, VHAR, HAR-KS and a GNN-HAR for more than half of 24 global indices at 1-, 5- and 22-day horizons, and stays in the Model Confidence Set for more than 20 of them `2410.22706v3` (pp.19-20).
5. Which spillover network you build decides whether it tracks crises: a Pearson-correlation graph stays calm through the 2008 and COVID-19 spikes, while a Diebold-Yilmaz magnetic-Laplacian graph rises with them `2410.22706v3` (p.3).
6. Volatility persistence is not constant: a time-varying extended-Wold model beats a time-varying HAR at 22 days with relative RMSE 0.807 for crude oil, 0.923 for natural gas and 0.880 for gasoline, and its edge weakens in the calm 1993-1999 sample `2402.01354v2` (pp.20,24).
7. A six-parameter restricted factor GARCH reproduces S&P covariances at RMSE 2.1390 against 1.4018 for DCC and 3.0633 for DECO, yields daily betas, and shows the leverage effect is a filtering artifact rather than a driver `1609.07051v6` (pp.1,14,18).
8. Implied volatility is not informationally efficient around jumps: after a negative return jump the at-the-money implied volatility rises an additional 0.218% over the next 60 minutes, and the drift is asymmetric across moneyness, with out-of-the-money calls converging immediately `1810.12200v1` (pp.3,13-14).
9. Equity-index option risk premia are negative for deep out-of-the-money calls because of unspanned jump and local-time risk: average weekly straddle returns are -10% and 3% out-of-the-money call returns are -59%, with upside and downside jump-crossing premia statistically at par `2303.16371v1` (pp.15-16).
10. Most price jumps are endogenous: only about 4.3% of 37,452 jumps in 301 US stocks fall within three minutes of news, 60% mean-revert, and co-jump size follows a power law with exponent about 0.95 `2404.16467v1` (pp.3,6,8).
11. Introducing CSI 300 index futures coincided with lower spot volatility (daily standard deviation 2.4603 to 1.4171) and weaker asymmetry (TGARCH bad-news coefficient 0.08833 to 0.07492), but the paper also reports negative post-break GARCH coefficients that are a specification warning `2109.15060v1` (pp.10,15-16).
12. Two market-level state variables separate from the price-index volatility: cross-sectional intrinsic entropy varies at least ten times more than index volatility estimators with index betas of roughly 0.06-0.45 `2205.00104v1` (pp.15,19), and a rank-invariant first-passage roughness exponent gives 0.8989 for rough Bergomi against 0.5635 for Heston on the VIX `2512.02352v3` (pp.8,12).

## 1. Point-forecast accuracy versus risk accuracy

`2310.01063v1` combines four GARCH specifications (GARCH, EGARCH, GJR-GARCH, APARCH) with a three-layer GRU network and tests on S&P 500, Bitcoin and gold daily returns over 2707 observations each (p.11). On the S&P 500 the hybrid cuts MSE from 0.0575 to 0.0159 for the skewed-t GARCH and from 0.1350 to 0.0152 for the skewed-t EGARCH, with Diebold-Mariano p-values below 0.05 throughout (p.13). On Bitcoin the pure GARCH MSE of 5.6795 falls to 0.4042 and the APARCH-GRU reaches 0.3818 against 5.835 for the plain APARCH (pp.16,18). The gain stops at the point forecast. For the S&P 500 the best 5% Value-at-Risk hit ratio comes from the GJR-GARCH-GRU with normal and skewed-t noise, the best 1% from the plain APARCH with skewed-t noise, and that plain APARCH is the only selected model that passes Kupiec, Christoffersen and the Expected Shortfall backtest together (p.15). For Bitcoin it is the sheer APARCH models, not the hybrids, that deliver the best Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall (p.18). The authors' own reading is that the GKYZ target chosen for the neural component is deficient for risk assessment and that the loss function should be redefined for the risk task (p.21). The repo's separation of concerns matches this: point forecast error and a risk quantile are scored by different functions, so a hybrid that only lowers MSE buys nothing for the risk leg unless the loss is changed.

## 2. Estimator choice: the range estimators are a different measurement

The repo's `volatility_models.py` offers close-to-close semivariance, Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Yang-Zhang, EWMA and GARCH(1,1), and `overlays.py` exposes a `volatility_estimator` switch (default `close`, alternatives `ewma` and `garch`) that only affects the regime label's volatility leg and the position scale. The slice says the choice is not cosmetic. `2205.00104v1` runs the Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Rogers-Satchell and Yang-Zhang estimators side by side against close-to-close on the S&P 500 over 2001-2022 and every range estimator sits below the close-to-close mean at every window tested: at a 60-day interval with a 20-day moving average close-to-close is 0.00853 against 0.00657 (Rogers-Satchell) and 0.00726 (Yang-Zhang), and at 5295 days it is 0.01018 against 0.00779 and 0.00865 (pp.14-16, Table 3). The ordering is stable and the gap is one to three tenths of a volatility point. `2504.20116v1` arrives at the same object from the leverage side: it estimates AR(1)-GARCH(1,1) on SPY and finds a near-zero but statistically significant mean-reversion coefficient (phi = -0.0490) beside a GARCH persistence sum of alpha + beta = 0.9716, and it shows the compounding effect of a leveraged product is driven by autocorrelation and volatility clustering, not by a single "volatility drag" number (pp.13-14). The practical consequence for this repo is that switching the default estimator changes the level of every downstream percentile and risk quantile, so the switch must be recorded in the read's `basis` string and the percentile must be ranked against windows measured the same way.

## 3. Persistence is time-varying, not structural

`2402.01354v2` decomposes energy-futures realized volatility into persistence scales of 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128-plus days whose mix changes smoothly over time (p.15). Oil volatility of 2014-2022 is driven by 64- to 128-day shocks that generate more than 50% of the variance around COVID-19, then by 2- to 4-day transitory shocks accounting for about 35-40% after February 2022, while the calm 1993-1999 period is transitory-driven throughout (pp.16-17,22-23). Its forecasting model beats a time-varying HAR at 22 days with relative RMSE 0.807 (crude oil), 0.923 (natural gas) and 0.880 (gasoline), and its advantage is smaller and often insignificant in the calm sample (pp.20,24). `2109.15060v1` gives the same message through a market-structure shock: a GARCH(1,1) on the CSI 300 reports an ARCH-plus-GARCH sum of 0.9939, near-integrated, and the authors report that both coefficients change sign after index futures begin trading (pp.14-15). A near-unit-root persistence parameter estimated over a whole sample is therefore a summary of a moving mixture, and the repo's single `garch11_fit` long-run volatility should be read as a sample average, not a structural constant.

## 4. Implied versus realized: lagged, asymmetric, negatively priced

`1810.12200v1` tests whether option markets incorporate jump news at once, using minute-by-minute S&P 500 index options over 2006-2010 and a Lee-Mykland jump test (p.2). They do not. After a negative return jump the at-the-money implied volatility rises an additional 0.218% over the following 60 minutes beyond the simultaneous move, and the drift is persistent and directed, while the initial reaction after a positive jump largely reverts within the hour (p.3). The asymmetry is also across moneyness: implied volatility from out-of-the-money calls converges within the jump window, while at-the-money and out-of-the-money-put implied volatilities keep drifting (pp.13-14). `2303.16371v1` supplies the economics, using S&P 500 weekly options and farther-dated contracts over 415 weekly cycles from January 2011 to December 2018 (p.13): average straddle excess returns are -10% unconditionally, 3% out-of-the-money call excess returns are -59%, 3% out-of-the-money put excess returns are -61%, bootstrap intervals put the upside and downside jump-crossing premia statistically at par, and 88-day options imply negative local-time risk premia (pp.15-17). The decomposition rests on Tanaka's formula and is model-free, so the conclusion is not a parametric artifact. For this repo it means a volatility-risk-premium read built as implied minus realized carries a known wedge: realized range volatility understates the level the option market prices, and the residual is not noise but a signed premium.

## 5. Jumps: mostly endogenous, mean-reverting, co-jumping

`2404.16467v1` builds a wavelet-scattering embedding of each jump's return profile, 42 coefficients from 119 one-minute rescaled returns in a two-hour window, and clusters them without labels (p.4). Applied to 301 US stocks from January 2015 to December 2022 within the 10:30-15:00 window, it identifies 37,452 jumps, of which 16,127 belong to 2534 co-jumps and 21,325 are single (p.3). Only about 4.3% of jumps fall within three minutes of a news item; 60% have a positive mean-reversion score, so the price partly reverses the jump (pp.3,6); the co-jump size distribution is a power law with exponent about 0.95 and a cutoff near 100 stocks (p.8). Because the embedding is defined for any time series, the same machinery can classify a jump by its own return profile rather than by a news timestamp. `2503.19767v1` contributes the standard realized-variance jump decomposition using median realized volatility and a signed jump (p.14), and `1810.12200v1` and `2303.16371v1` price the jump channel. Together they say a jump detector that only checks a news feed will label the majority of real jumps as unexplained, and a risk layer that assumes mean-reversion will be right about 60% of the time and wrong about the rest.

## 6. Cross-sectional and graph structure, and a market-wide state variable

`2410.22706v3` is the clearest statement that volatility is a cross-sectional object: the Diebold-Yilmaz framework supplies a directed spillover network, the magnetic Laplacian preserves direction, and a graph Fourier transform moves the system into a spectral domain where a HAR filter is applied before an inverse transform (pp.13-15). Its model is selected by the Model Confidence Set for more than 20 of 24 indices and achieves the lowest MSE and MAE for more than half at 1, 5 and 22 days, while the graph-signal-energy diagnostic is model-agnostic and can be computed before any forecast (pp.19-20). `1609.07051v6` provides the parametric counterpart for a single market: on 356 S&P constituents over 1995-2013 it restricts the conditional covariance to one market eigenvalue plus N-1 degenerate equal factors, making the inverse analytic and leaving six GARCH parameters (p.1); the empirical covariance's largest eigenvalue is 104 against an order-one bulk (p.5), and the restricted model's covariance RMSE of 2.1390 is competitive against DCC's 1.4018, DECO's 3.0633 and OG-GARCH's 0.9513 (p.14). `2205.00104v1` adds a market-wide state variable that is not an index: cross-sectional intrinsic entropy built from every traded symbol's OHLC and volume on the NYSE and NASDAQ has variance at least an order of magnitude above every index estimator at every window, and the index volatility's beta against it is 0.06-0.45, that is 50-90% lower volatility risk (pp.15,19). The defensible extraction is that a broad-market volatility reading should be compared both to its own index and to a cross-sectional dispersion measure, because the two can diverge sharply.

## 7. Roughness as a rank-invariant path diagnostic

`2512.02352v3` introduces the forward visibility horizon of a horizontal visibility graph and proves it equals the first-passage time on any path without ties, with an exact i.i.d. survival law of 1/k and infinite mean and variance (pp.3-4). Combining that identity with persistence theory for fractional Brownian motion gives a single tail exponent theta(H) = 1 - H (p.5). On 10,000 Monte-Carlo paths of length 2^16 the estimator recovers the prediction within one cross-replicate standard deviation for H <= 0.2 (p.8, Table 1), and on four calibrated volatility models it gives 0.8989 for rough Bergomi against 0.8409 for FIGARCH, 0.7234 for GARCH(1,1) and 0.5635 for Heston, separating rough Bergomi from Heston by 0.3355 (pp.11-12, Table 3). Applied to daily FRED VIX over 2000-2026 it returns 0.910 +/- 0.19 across 45 four-year windows against an overlapping-window i.i.d. null mean of 1.160 (p.14). The caveat the authors state is that the separation from FIGARCH is only about 0.06, so on samples shorter than a decade the statistic may not distinguish roughness from long memory. This is a candidate replacement for the rescaled-range Hurst exponent the repo computes today, with a closed-form null rather than a calibration curve.

## Caveats

No paper in this read set establishes that any of these forecast or estimator improvements transfers to this platform's venues or instruments. The GARCH-GRU result is on three assets, two of which are not equities, and its own authors decline to claim the point-forecast gain reaches risk. The sentiment results are single-market (US equities) and single-period. The CSI 300 result is one emerging market over one listing event and reports negative post-break GARCH coefficients that a specification-sensitive reader should treat as a warning about the estimation window rather than a stable parameter. The element of the slice with the clearest structural warning is the volatility-drag paper: its positive expected compounding effect is derived under an estimated AR(1)-GARCH(1,1) whose sign is explicitly not guaranteed in theory, so its simulated compounding numbers (for example SPY SSO 0.0744) are model-conditional. The jump classification is one US equity sample over 2015-2022 with a fixed intraday window and a 250-stock exclusion rule, so the 4.3% news-relation figure is a lower bound on exogenous jumps, not a reflexive fraction. The option-premia evidence is index options over one eight-year window and is model-free in the two components it prices, but it is still one market. The roughness estimator is the least settled: its separation from long memory is about 0.06 and its rough-Bergomi prediction rests on a stated unproven persistence hypothesis. Nothing here was verified by running the repo's calculators against these samples; the landing sites are file and symbol references only. Single-country, single-period, model-only and overlapping-window limits apply throughout, and no result was net of transaction costs except where the source paper states it.

## Papers read in depth

- `2310.01063v1` - Combining Deep Learning and GARCH Models for Financial Volatility and Risk Forecasting (2023). Hybrid GARCH-GRU cuts point volatility MSE by an order of magnitude on three assets but does not improve Value-at-Risk or Expected Shortfall; the loss function, not the model, is the binding constraint.
- `2503.19767v1` - Forecasting U.S. equity market volatility with attention and sentiment to the economy (2025). Sentiment to macro announcements helps on extreme days and hurts on normal days; single-market and text-data dependent.
- `2410.22706v3` - Graph Signal Processing for Global Stock Market Realized Volatility Forecasting (2024). A directional spillover graph beats HAR benchmarks on 24 indices; the graph-signal-energy diagnostic is the transferable part.
- `2402.01354v2` - Predicting the volatility of major energy commodity prices: the dynamic persistence model (2024). Time-varying persistence beats a time-varying HAR at long horizons; three energy futures only.
- `1609.07051v6` - Multivariate GARCH with dynamic beta (2016). A six-parameter factor GARCH is competitive on S&P covariances and yields daily betas; the factor restriction is strong and the sample is one market.
- `1810.12200v1` - Option market (in)efficiency and implied volatility dynamics after return jumps (2018). At-the-money implied volatility drifts for 60 minutes after negative jumps; single-market and no post-cost edge.
- `2404.16467v1` - Riding Wavelets: A Method to Discover New Classes of Price Jumps (2024). Unsupervised wavelet embedding finds mean-reverting, trend and anticipatory jump classes and mostly endogenous co-jumps; one US equity sample.
- `2303.16371v1` - Dark Matter in (Volatility and) Equity Option Risk Premiums (2023). Deep out-of-the-money calls and straddles earn negative premia via unspanned jump and local-time risk; one index over eight years.
- `2109.15060v1` - Stock index futures trading impact on spot price volatility. The CSI 300 studied with a TGARCH model (2021). Index futures listing coincides with lower spot volatility and asymmetry; the reported negative GARCH coefficients are a specification warning.
- `2504.20116v1` - Compounding Effects in Leveraged ETFs: Beyond the Volatility Drag Paradigm (2025). The compounding effect depends on autocorrelation and volatility clustering, not a single drag term; the sign is model-conditional.
- `2205.00104v1` - The Cross-Sectional Intrinsic Entropy. A Comprehensive Stock Market Volatility Estimator (2022). A market-wide entropy reading varies at least ten times more than index estimators and shows 50-90% lower index volatility risk; two US exchanges, one period.
- `2512.02352v3` - First-passage horizons in horizontal visibility graphs: a rank-invariant estimator of path roughness for rough volatility models (2025). A closed-form-null roughness exponent separates rough from classical volatility on synthetic data; separation from FIGARCH is small.
