# Bubbles, crashes and criticality: the defensible output is a distribution over a regime change, not a crash date and not a short signal

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief reads 153 harvested records on bubbles, crashes and criticality, spanning 2000-2026, of which 55 carry a journal reference and 24 appeared from 2021 onward. From 40 screened candidates, twelve papers are read in depth at the PDF. The lens is TradingAgents, a deterministic research and analysis layer: calculators under tradingagents/strategies/ exposed as LangChain tools, consumed by the score engines, gated by enable_* config keys and written into the decision contract. That layer already ships regime machinery - cusum, ewma_control, bocpd, hmm_filtered_regime, regime_score, triadic_stress, tail_risk and a drawdown gate - so this brief is written to add what the repo lacks and to name what it should stop claiming. Where the slice touches regime change points and tail risk generally, it defers to Strategies/books/07-regime-changepoint.md and Strategies/books/08-risk-tail.md and only adds what those do not carry.

## What this covers

The slice runs from the Johansen-Ledoit-Sornette (JLS) definition of a bubble as faster-than-exponential growth decorated by log-periodic oscillations, through the re-parameterisation that made its calibration deterministic, to the published out-of-sample record and its failures, and then to two diagnostics that never look at price dynamics at all: right-tailed unit-root exuberance tests on valuation ratios, and recurrence- or motif-based indicators on baskets and networks. The single most consequential finding for this repo is negative. The defensible output of the price-dynamics method is a distribution over the critical time, not a crash date: on synthetic series with noise at 5% of the largest log-price, a true critical time of 300 days is recovered at 296.07 with a standard deviation of 20.44 over 2000 estimates, an uncertainty about three times smaller than the 60 days separating the last observation from the critical time (`1107.3171v3`, p.14-16), while about one bubble in three ends smoothly rather than crashing (`1001.0265v2`, p.7). Any research surface that emits a dated crash call, or that turns a bubble flag into a short, is publishing a claim the literature does not support.

The second consequential finding is that the repo's own shift detectors are drift and variance instruments, not crash-early-warning instruments. On twelve Chinese bank-crisis days the standard critical-slowing-down triplet - variance, lag-1 autocorrelation and mean power spectrum - passed its sensitivity screen 0/12, 2/12 and 2/12 times respectively, and its significance screen 1/12, 6/12 and 5/12 times, while a multiplex recurrence-network mutual-information peak covered 9 of the 12 days with 3 exactly coinciding (`2310.10283v1`, p.12, p.15). `tradingagents/strategies/regime.py::cusum`, `ewma_control` and `bocpd` should keep doing the job they are demonstrably good at, detecting sustained mean and variance shifts, and stop being described as warnings of a crash.

Selection rule: a paper had to carry a reproducible quantitative diagnostic of a bubble, a crash or a critical transition - an LPPL calibration, a right-tailed unit-root test, or a network or recurrence indicator - together with either an out-of-sample record or an explicit mechanism; narrative, policy and undated-prophecy records were dropped.

## The short answer

1. A bubble here is a dynamic regime, not a mispricing: faster-than-exponential growth with log-periodic oscillations, and the method says nothing about fundamental value (`1404.2140v1`, p.19-21).
2. Calibration is now deterministic enough to automate: expanding the cosine into C1 = C cos(phi) and C2 = C sin(phi) leaves three nonlinear parameters and removes the quasi-periodic multi-minimum landscape that had forced taboo search or genetic algorithms (`1108.0099v3`, p.10-12).
3. Over six-month windows on the SSE Composite from July 1999 to May 2011 the cost function never showed more than three distinguishable local minima, and most windows qualifying as bubble-regime showed exactly one (`1108.0099v3`, p.12-13).
4. The output is a distribution over the critical time: over 2000 estimates a true t_c of 300 days is recovered at 296.07 (standard deviation 20.44) under Gaussian noise and 295.15 (20.81) under Student-t noise (`1107.3171v3`, p.14-16).
5. About one bubble in three lands smoothly, and a crash can also occur before the critical time with smaller probability, so only probabilistic readings are supported (`1001.0265v2`, p.7).
6. The model covers endogenous crashes only: of 49 drawdown outliers, 25 are endogenous, 22 exogenous and 2 tied to the Japanese anti-bubble, so roughly one major crash in three is outside its premises (`1107.3171v3`, p.9-10).
7. The strongest ex-ante case in the slice is oil: an 80% confidence interval of 17 May to 14 July 2008 for the peak, with the actual peak on 3 July and the steep descent starting 11 July (`1001.0265v2`, p.8).
8. Long-horizon directional skill is mostly ambient drift: S&P 500 anti-bubble predictions hit 53% at a one-day horizon and 90-95% beyond 120 days, which the authors state reflects the bear trend rather than the model (`physics/0301023v2`, p.14).
9. Timing is unstable across the series of one episode: for Royal Ahold the equity fit puts t_c at 2004/06/19, the post-bubble fit at 1996/07/24 and the sales fit at 2002/05/20 for a February 2003 collapse (`cond-mat/0403563v1`, p.8-10).
10. A different diagnostic family gives a different answer: over 1999-2000 the right-tailed GSADF finds exuberance in the NIFTY 50 (3.728618, p = 0.0000) but not in the S&P BSE SENSEX (0.148245, p = 0.4640), so the two families disagree about which index was in a bubble (`2207.13444v1`, p.3).
11. The dangerous nodes in a crash network are small and highly nested: the critical market confidence obeys alpha_c = 1 - c, and randomising the bipartite stock-investor network steepens that to alpha_c = 1 - 2c, making resilience a portfolio-overlap property rather than a size property (`1908.07244v1`, p.6-7, p.16-18).
12. Structural early warnings are measurable but null-model dependent: a multiplex recurrence network flagged 9 of 12 bank-crisis days against 0-2 of 12 for the standard triplet, while the Dutch interbank triadic inversion backdated the 2008 break to early 2005 and disappeared for several motifs under the stricter reciprocal configuration model (`2310.10283v1`, p.12, p.15; `1403.4460v2`, p.7-9).

## 1. What the LPPL model claims, and what it refuses to claim

`1404.2140v1` states the definition with unusual clarity: a bubble is a period of unsustainable growth in which the growth rate itself grows, so the price follows a faster-than-exponential path that ends at a critical point where the model stops having a solution; log-periodic oscillations are added as a second, independently motivated signature, and the full formula carries seven parameters (`1404.2140v1`, p.10, p.18). Positive feedback is the ingredient that produces the runaway: the paper contrasts exponential growth, which can continue indefinitely, with hyperbolic growth, which reaches a point of instability where the price process ceases to exist (p.10). Bitcoin before its November 2013 correction is the paper's worked example, fitted at an average annualised growth rate of almost 700%, a number that is too high for most of the period and too low for the final stage precisely because the growth rate was itself growing (p.11).

The mechanism is described in two classes: a technical class - option hedging, portfolio insurance, trend following, procyclical bank financing, stop-losses, market-maker spread optimisation and mark-to-market regulatory capital - and a behavioural class built on imitation and herding, with the argument that imitation is close to optimal under serious uncertainty, which is why bubbles recur (p.12). That mechanism story is not calibrated to observed flow anywhere in the slice; a platform that imports the diagnostic should not import the mechanism as a generator. The one operational sentence that matters most for this repo is explicit: the methodology identifies dynamic situations that are not sustainable and has, in itself, nothing to say about any possible mispricing (p.20-21). The authors separate assets that are fundamentally over- or undervalued from assets that develop a positive or negative bubble, and they say the two coincide only sometimes (p.20-21).

The operational footprint is an ensemble over window starts: because bubbles form on all time scales and the time base is unknown, the method scans many window sizes and reports an alarm index with a 50% crash-probability line and a broader expected-correction band, not a single fit (`1404.2140v1`, p.19). At the time of writing the ETH Financial Crisis Observatory ran that scan over 25,000 financial time series daily (p.19, p.22).

## 2. Calibration: from metaheuristics to a bounded three-parameter search

The methodological core of the slice is `1108.0099v3`. The original LPPL needs three linear and four nonlinear parameters, and because the three linear ones can be slaved to the nonlinear ones the search is four-dimensional with a quasi-periodic, multi-minimum cost surface that historically required taboo search or a genetic algorithm and even then gave no convergence guarantee (p.4, p.7). Expanding the cosine into C1 = C cos(phi) and C2 = C sin(phi) turns the model into four linear and three nonlinear parameters, eliminates the quasi-periodicity, and leaves a smooth surface where controlled local search suffices (p.10-11). A second subordination step slaves m and omega to t_c so fit quality can be read directly as a function of the critical time: the reduced cost function F2(t_c) carries fitted functions m-hat(t_c) and omega-hat(t_c), which lets the constraint region be drawn on the same axes (p.12-14).

The empirical support is one index and it is honest about its limits. Scanning six-month windows across the SSE Composite from July 1999 to May 2011, the authors never found more than three clearly distinguishable local minima of F1(t_c, m, omega) with t_c ranging from one to ninety days beyond the window end; most windows qualifying as bubble-regime gave one minimum, and a second or third minimum usually corresponded to a poor fit (p.12-13). Launching no more than twenty local searches from random starts in 0.1 <= m <= 0.9 and 6 <= omega <= 13 was enough to find them (p.13).

`1107.3171v3` supplies the validity filters that should gate any such fit. The exponent m must lie in [0, 1] because m < 1 keeps the integral of the crash hazard rate finite and less than one for all times up to t_c - which is exactly why it is rational to stay invested - while m > 0 keeps the price finite at t_c (p.5-6). The van Bothmer-Meister non-negative-risk condition gives b = -Bm - |C| sqrt(m^2 + omega^2) >= 0, which most current work treats as a basic filter (p.7). That condition is not cosmetic: when it fails, the fitted LPPL has a negative expected slope at some times, which contradicts the martingale condition under a non-negative hazard rate, and the authors concede that the objection is well taken (p.7-8). `1108.0099v3` adds the compiled stylised ranges 0.1 <= m <= 0.9, 6 <= omega <= 13, |C| < 1 and B < 0, where the omega band exists so the oscillations are neither fast enough to fit noise nor slow enough to contribute to the trend (p.6).

## 3. The ex-ante record, and the one-in-three smooth landing

`1001.0265v2` is the clearest documented ex-ante case in the slice. The 2008 oil bubble was flagged with an 80% confidence interval of 17 May to 14 July 2008 for the end of the unsustainable phase, using data through the last week of May; the actual peak was 3 July and the steep descent began 11 July, both inside the interval (p.8). For the 2009 Shanghai bubble, a prediction submitted on 10 July 2009 gave 20%/80% quantiles of 17-27 July and 10%/90% quantiles of 10 July to 10 August; re-run five days later it tightened to an 80% probability of 19 July to 3 August, and Chinese stocks fell sharply on 29 July with the Shanghai index down 22% in August (p.8-9). The same paper supplies the crucial negative result: a bubble can land smoothly approximately one-third of the time, according to the authors' own preliminary investigations, so "only probabilistic forecasts can be developed" (p.7).

The same paper shows how to turn detection into a supervised problem and how to score it. A negative bubble is defined with the same power law and the sign of B reversed, so a positive B gives a downward-accelerating log-price and m keeps the constraint 0 < m < 1; the accelerated decline is a regime that ends in a rebound rather than a crash (p.10). For the rebound study the S&P 500 series from 1950-01-05 to 2009-06-03 is cut into 11,662 windows with start and end steps of 50 calendar days, a minimum window of 110 days and a maximum of 1500 days; fits before 1975-01-01 form the learning set, a rebound is a local minimum over a window extending 200 days on each side, and the out-of-sample quality is read off an error diagram whose anti-diagonal is the random-prediction line (p.12-14).

`2607.21826v1` is the most careful application because it expresses each signal as a date, a standard deviation and a signal count rather than a date alone: Bitcoin is 14Dec2017 +/- 3 with 50 signals under ordinary least squares and 15Dec2017 +/- 9 with 52 signals under generalised least squares, and it runs the LPPL and Phillips-Shi-Yu families side by side with the same multi-window scanning and a genetic-algorithm global search (p.6-7, p.12). It also reports its own failure mode: a 28% slump over two weeks in September 2017 produced no valid LPPL signal and is classified as a local swing tied to exogenous news, which is the honest boundary of the method (p.7).

## 4. Where the point-dated forecasts over-reach

Three cases in the slice show the ceiling. `cond-mat/0403563v1` fits Royal Ahold across several decades and reports, on the same figure, an equity power-law fit with t_c = 2004/06/19 and m = -4.62, and a post-bubble log-periodic fit with t_c = 1996/07/24 and omega = 12.31 (p.9-10). The two critical times disagree by eight years, the equity fit points to a regime change in mid-2004, well over a year after the actual collapse on 24 February 2003, and the sales fit gives t_c = 2002/05/20 while the earnings-based t_c = 2002/09/28 is explicitly called not robust because quarterly earnings are prone to manipulation (p.8-10). The paper is candid that no precise timing can be claimed, that the rule of thumb is only that t_c is robust to about one year before a crash, and that for an individual company the timing is harder than for an index because of coupling to the market mood and to acquisition policy (p.10). Its constructive contribution is the "critical zone": after the super-exponential phase the price does not crash directly but passes through an oscillatory destabilisation regime that can last weeks, months or years, quantified with a Weidlich opinion-formation model whose neutral point is stable only while kappa < 1 + mu and beta < (1 - kappa)/gamma (p.11-12, p.18).

`1012.4118v1` forecasts the burst of the gold bubble for late April to early May 2011 from t_c = 2011.45, refined to May and then May-June 2011, with a target price of $1500-1700 in current dollars (p.6-7, p.10-11). Its fitted omega of 15.86 sits above the 2.9-15 range the authors themselves cite from Sornette (2004), it fits price rather than log-price because the log-price cost function showed no local minimum, and the paper contains no ex-post evaluation of the forecast and warns that a large intervention by the Federal Reserve or the People's Bank of China removes the foundation of the calculation (p.6, p.13). Its two cross-checks are worth keeping as method: the historical calibration shows the forecast critical time stabilises only about two months before the event, and the oil analogue gives a critical moment of 31 August 2008 for an actual crash starting 14 July 2008 under a rule of thumb of about 1.4 months (p.7-8).

`physics/0301023v2` shows how a high hit rate can be trend and not skill. Its Weierstrass-type extension reproduces five crashes in the 2000-2002 S&P 500 anti-bubble with three free nonlinear parameters and a residual r.m.s. of 0.0252 against 0.0325 for the single-harmonic fit, with the fitted t_c stabilising at 26 July 2000, m = 0.53 and omega = 11.43 (p.9-10). The retrospective prediction exercise then reports success rates rising from 53% at a one-day horizon to 90-95% beyond 120 days, and the authors state plainly that the high-horizon figure only reflects the overall downward trend and is not a qualifier of the model; they argue the plateaus near 75% between 20 and 70 days are the genuine predictive content (p.14). Their trading simulation over the same period gives daily Sharpe ratios of 0.1-0.14 against 0.06 for short-and-hold, with costs stated not to erase the effect (p.14-15).

## 5. The second diagnostic family: right-tailed unit-root exuberance tests

`2207.13444v1` occupies different ground. Rather than fitting price dynamics, it applies the Phillips-Shi-Yu generalised sup augmented Dickey-Fuller test to price-earnings ratios of six Indian indices over the dot-com episode of 1999-2000, with critical values from 1000 Monte Carlo replications and lag order zero (p.2-3). The null is a unit root; rejection indicates an explosive, exuberant regime. The GSADF statistics are 3.728618 (p = 0.0000) for the NIFTY 50, 2.858897 (p = 0.0010) for the NIFTY 500, 2.484461 (p = 0.0290) for the S&P BSE 100, 0.653972 (p = 0.4920) for the NIFTY BANK and 0.148245 (p = 0.4640) for the S&P BSE SENSEX (p.3). Four of six indices show exuberance and two do not, which is the point: the two diagnostics in the slice - price-path LPPL and valuation-ratio unit root - answer different questions and can disagree about the same market in the same window.

Two defects in the paper are instructive rather than disqualifying. The GSADF value 3.728618 is printed for both the NIFTY 50 and the NIFTY NEXT 50, so the index-to-index comparison is not clean, and the date-stamping figure shows a two-month spike for the S&P BSE 100, which is the honest shape of the output: a window, not a date (p.3-5). `2607.21826v1` runs the same family on crypto and grades its alerts by confidence band: Bitcoin windows 11Dec2016-06Jan2017 and 21Mar2017-2May2017 at medium confidence and 5May2017 onward at high confidence, with the Ether table containing one window printed as 06Dec2017-14Mar2017, which ends before it starts (p.7, p.12). The banding is the useful part; the internal inconsistency is a reminder to validate any hand-assembled signal table before it enters a registry.

## 6. Criticality measured in structure: recurrence, nesting and triadic loops

The second half of the slice abandons price dynamics for structure. `1908.07244v1` builds a bipartite stock-investor network from Chinese mutual-fund holdings as of 30 June 2015 (87 fund companies, 2709 stocks) and studies the single-stock price limit as an individual risk-tolerance parameter c (p.6, p.19). The model yields an unexpected linear relation between the critical market confidence and the price limit, alpha_c = 1 - c, so raising the limit barely reduces the confidence needed to keep the system stable (p.6-7). The nodes that drive collapse are not the superspreaders of the contagion literature but relatively small stocks with high nestedness and branching: they share all their investors with more popular neighbours, absorb the first-round shock and broadcast it onward, and the simulated cascade runs from the periphery of the network toward its core before spreading out (p.9-13). Randomising the network steepens the boundary to alpha_c = 1 - 2c and reduces the abundance of high-nestedness nodes, which makes portfolio overlap, not size, the resilience variable (p.16-18).

`2310.10283v1` supplies the comparative numbers that matter most here. It builds multiplex recurrence networks from one-minute returns of 16 listed Chinese banks over 2005 trading days from 4 January 2012 to 30 October 2020, using the average mutual information across layers as the indicator (p.11). At a jump-test confidence of 0.001 it detects 12 systemic-volatility days: the mutual-information peaks cover 9 of them, with 3 exactly coinciding, and cover each peak plus the following nine days as the risk interval; the average edge-overlap indicator covers only 7 of 27 events at the 0.0025 level (p.12). Its benchmark is the recognised critical-slowing-down triplet, and that benchmark fails: variance passes the sensitivity screen 0/12 and the significance screen 1/12; mean power spectrum 2/12 and 6/12; lag-1 autocorrelation 2/12 and 5/12 (p.15). The topology adds a second read: maximum spanning trees at indicator peaks lack a dominant core node, while non-peak periods have a stable central node (p.15).

`1403.4460v2` generalises the same idea to a stationarity test and shows how much the answer depends on the null model. It builds maximum-entropy graph ensembles driven by local constraints and asks whether a real network is a typical member; the International Trade Network (six decades from 1950) is almost perfectly quasi-stationary, with all triadic z-scores within +/-3 once reciprocated degrees are constrained (p.4). The Dutch Interbank Network (44 quarterly snapshots, 1998-2008) is not: its z-score profiles do not collapse across snapshots, and four subperiods can be separated, with the largest structural change occurring between the second and third, that is at the start of the pre-crisis phase rather than at the onset of the crisis itself (p.5-6). Under the directed random graph the dyadic z-scores only collapse when the crisis is already manifest and give no early warning; under the configuration model they invert sign at the start of 2005, backdating the structural change three years before the 2008 crisis (p.7). Motif m = 5 loses its apparent early-warning character once reciprocated links are controlled for, whereas the directed loops - especially the unreciprocated ones - stay significant, and motif m = 9 identifies an even earlier phase from roughly 2000 to 2005 that the authors call the cyclic anomaly phase (p.8-9).

## 7. What the standard regime detectors do and do not deliver

Read together, the two halves of the slice give this repo a clear division of labour. `tradingagents/strategies/regime.py` already contains a tabular CUSUM for small sustained mean shifts, an EWMA control chart for slow drifts, a Bayesian online change-point detector with a hazard rate, a filtered hidden-Markov regime label and a spectral change read. Those are exactly the instruments that the critical-slowing-down benchmark used, and that benchmark failed as an early warning on the bank data (`2310.10283v1`, p.15). Nothing in the slice says the detectors do not work as change-point detectors; the slice says they are not crash warnings, and that a single-window level or volatility shift is the weakest of the available signals. The stronger signals in the slice are cross-sectional and relational: mutual information across many series (`2310.10283v1`, p.12), the sign pattern of a triadic profile against a null model (`1403.4460v2`, p.7-9), and portfolio overlap in a holdings network (`1908.07244v1`, p.16-18).

Two methodological points carry over directly. First, the window is a free parameter and single-window output is unreliable: the LPPL work is explicit that an ensemble over start dates is recommended because the start of a bubble is a fuzzy and degenerate concept (`1107.3171v3`, p.12), and the stationarity work is explicit that the answer changes with the null model (`1403.4460v2`, p.8). Second, the same episode cannot be described by fitted parameters alone: `1107.3171v3` concedes that the taboo-search-plus-Levenberg-Marquardt method without a sound starting value will produce spurious fits trapped at a search boundary, that the quality of a fit must also be checked by visualisation against the data, and that the whole exercise is an advocate's best practice rather than a neutral audit (`1107.3171v3`, p.13-14). `1108.0099v3` removes the metaheuristic requirement, but not the need for the filters, for a variance-stable fit on log-price rather than price, and for a check that the fitted series is not simply a smooth trend (`1107.3171v3`, p.12).

## 8. What is actually established

Four things survive scrutiny. First, the faster-than-exponential growth signature is measurable and the LPPL functional form fits bubble episodes across equities, indices, commodities, real estate and crypto (`1404.2140v1`, p.10-11; `1012.4118v1`, p.6; `2607.21826v1`, p.6-7). Second, the calibration problem is solved well enough to automate, with a three-parameter nonlinear search, an ensemble over window starts, and explicit validity filters (`1108.0099v3`, p.10-13; `1107.3171v3`, p.7, p.12). Third, the output is a probability distribution over a regime change with a timing uncertainty on the order of 20 days on synthetic data and roughly a one-in-three chance of no crash at all (`1107.3171v3`, p.14-16; `1001.0265v2`, p.7). Fourth, structural criticality is a separate and shorter-horizon signal family, measurable in recurrence networks, portfolio nesting and triadic motifs, but its output is sensitive to the null model and it has been demonstrated on one country and a handful of event days at a time (`2310.10283v1`, p.12, p.15; `1908.07244v1`, p.16-18; `1403.4460v2`, p.7-9). What is not established anywhere in the slice is that any of these diagnostics supports a dated crash call, a direction, or a position size.

## Caveats

Most of the slice is case study, and the case studies are single-market and single-period. `cond-mat/0403563v1` is two fits of one Dutch company read retrospectively; `physics/0301023v2` is one index over one anti-bubble whose forward prediction is unscored in the paper; `1012.4118v1` is one commodity, uses an omega above the range its authors cite, and contains no ex-post evaluation; `2207.13444v1` covers one two-year dot-com window with price-earnings ratios only, prints a duplicated test statistic and uses a small index set; `2607.21826v1` covers about 13.5 months of two crypto assets, ends before most of its signals can be scored, and prints at least one reversed window; `2310.10283v1` is one country's listed banks over 2012-2020 with 12 crisis days defined by a jump test at a chosen confidence, and reports only 50 simulation runs for its parameter selection; `1908.07244v1` uses mutual-fund holdings while individuals dominate Chinese trading, which the authors call a limit on predictive power; `1403.4460v2` is one interbank network of 44 quarterly snapshots whose counterparty data is regulatory and unavailable here. The LPPL papers are largely authored by the method's own proponents, so their extra-sample record should be treated as advocacy until independently reproduced. No paper in the slice charges transaction costs to the detection-to-position path, and the window counts overlap heavily, so the window-level statistics are not independent samples. Two of the twelve findings have no research-layer landing in the strict sense: the ownership-network results (`1908.07244v1`, `1403.4460v2`) assume a bipartite holdings or exposure graph that this repository does not hold - no fund-holdings or interbank-exposure table exists under tradingagents/dataflows/ - so they can only be approximated by the correlation- and return-based surfaces named in the table. The single-country, single-period limits mean that none of the reported hit rates should be treated as a base rate for a different market or era.

## Papers read in depth

- `1107.3171v3` - Clarifications to Questions and Criticisms on the Johansen-Ledoit-Sornette Bubble Model (2011). The best single statement of the bounds, the endogenous-only scope, the validity filters and the synthetic recovery of the critical time; it is an advocate's document rather than a neutral audit.
- `1108.0099v3` - A Stable and Robust Calibration Scheme of the Log-Periodic Power Law Model (2011). The re-parameterisation that removes quasi-periodicity and cuts the nonlinear search to three parameters; the empirical support is one index.
- `1404.2140v1` - Financial bubbles: mechanisms and diagnostics (2014). The clearest operational definition, the mechanism taxonomy and the FCO ensemble-scanning setup; white-paper detail with no falsification tests.
- `1001.0265v2` - Diagnosis and Prediction of Tipping Points in Financial Markets: Crashes and Rebounds (2010). The clearest ex-ante record in the slice plus the negative-bubble rebound method and error-diagram scoring; the successful cases are two.
- `1012.4118v1` - Log-Periodic Oscillation Analysis and Possible Burst of the Gold Bubble in April - June 2011 (2010). A dated, falsifiable gold forecast with two independent cross-checks; omega is out of the cited range and no outcome is evaluated.
- `cond-mat/0403563v1` - Bubble, Critical Zone and the Crash of Royal Ahold (2004). Couples super-exponential sales and earnings, a post-bubble destabilisation regime and an opinion-formation model; a single retrofitted company and two fits that disagree on the critical time limit it.
- `physics/0301023v2` - Renormalization Group Analysis of the 2000-2002 anti-bubble in the US S&P 500 index (2003). A Weierstrass-type extension reproduces five crashes and reports retroactive success rates and one forward forecast; the high-horizon skill is trend, as the authors concede.
- `2207.13444v1` - Interrogation of A Bubble in the Indian Market (2022). A right-tailed unit-root test on Indian index valuation ratios; a duplicated statistic and a two-year window weaken the index-to-index comparison.
- `2607.21826v1` - Are cryptocurrencies real financial bubbles? Evidence from quantitative analyses (2026). Runs LPPL and Phillips-Shi-Yu side by side on Bitcoin and Ether with dated signals and signal counts; the sample ends before most signals are scored and one PSY window is reversed.
- `1908.07244v1` - The emergence of critical stocks in market crash (2019). Shows that small, highly nested stocks drive cascades and that portfolio overlap sets resilience; the investor network is mutual funds only.
- `2310.10283v1` - Unveiling Early Warning Signals of Systemic Risks in Banks: A Recurrence Network-Based Approach (2023). A high-frequency indicator that preserves non-stationarity and beats the standard critical-slowing-down benchmark; one country and 12 crisis days.
- `1403.4460v2` - Stationarity, non-stationarity and early warning signals in economic networks (2014). A maximum-entropy ensemble test that separates a stationary trade network from an out-of-equilibrium interbank network and dates a structural break three years early; the result is null-model dependent and the exposure data is not publicly reproducible.
