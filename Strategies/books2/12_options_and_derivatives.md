# Options and derivative instruments: every implied number this repo publishes is a vendor IV mapped through a zero-rate Black-76, and the surface literature says to price the carry and distrust the fit

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief covers the options-and-derivatives slice of the local arXiv harvest for `Strategies/books2/`: what an option price encodes beyond direction, how the implied-volatility and variance surface is inverted and calibrated, where put-call parity stops being a free lunch, and what the derivative-instrument evidence says about instrument scope. It reads twelve papers from the 96-record slice spanning 2008-2026, 17 of which carry a journal reference, screened from the ranked 40-record shortlist. It is written for the research and analysis layer of this repository - the deterministic calculators in `tradingagents/strategies/*.py`, their `@tool` bindings in `tradingagents/agents/utils/*_tools.py`, the analyst prompts and the score engines - and it deliberately stays on what to compute, what to distrust, and which number to stop publishing.

## What this covers

The slice runs from the inversion of the Black-Scholes formula itself, through the joint calibration of the implied-vol surface and the variance term structure, into the parity-arbitrage question and the funding frictions that survive it, and finally to the forecasting and instrument-scope studies. The single most consequential finding for this repo is that a clean price-space identity hides a systematic carry wedge: SPX and RUT put-call parity residuals against traded futures sit in roughly [-0.1, +0.1] while the option-implied discount factor sits a mean 36.91 bp above OIS, positive in 98.4% of observations (`2604.19604v6`, pp.2, 9). This repo's own parity screen assumes `r = 0, q = 0` and reads last traded prices, so by construction it can only ever report a price-space violation and can never see that wedge (`tradingagents/agents/utils/analysis_tools.py::get_parity_screen`). The second most consequential finding is that the whole pile of implied quantities this repo derives - `iv_skew`, `implied_move_pct`, `volatility_risk_premium`, the recovered risk-neutral density - is computed from a vendor `implied_volatility` field, not from a fitted surface, and one of the papers here (`2301.11078v1`) shows exactly how a closed-form option formula with an uncalibrated free parameter fails as a measurement.

## The short answer

1. Black-Scholes implied volatility has an exact closed form: implied total variance is a quantile of a generalized inverse Gaussian law, w = F_GIG^-1(c; 1/2, 1/4, k^2) for the out-of-the-money branch k > 0 (Eq. 11, p.5), so variance and not volatility is the coordinate of the inversion `2604.24480v4`.
2. The implementation is not a root-solve but a quantile call, and it is roughly 3x faster than the Let's Be Rational reference (about 59 ns vs 180 ns per evaluation) on a simulated grid only `2604.24480v4`.
3. Fitting the implied-vol surface does not pin the variance term structure: surface-only calibration leaves model-implied VIX-term-structure errors of 10% to 20% of level, fixed by adding a penalty with a trade-off parameter alpha in [0, 1] (Eq. 2, p.4) `2509.08096v1`.
4. With jumps the variance-swap multiplier departs from two and the gap to squared VIX is a third-moment term, VS - VIX^2 about minus lambda times E[J^3]/3, so a positive gap implies a negatively skewed jump distribution (Eq. 21, p.14) `2509.08096v1`.
5. Put-call parity residuals against traded futures are tightly compressed around zero in both SPX and RUT, yet the option-implied discount factor sits a mean 36.91 bp above OIS, positive in 98.4% of observations `2604.19604v6`.
6. That carry wedge is not cross-sectional noise: the identification fit has median R^2 of 0.9999999 (SPX) and 0.9999995 (RUT), and a path-risk term volatility x sqrt(maturity) plus bid-ask and financial-condition variables explains about 31% of it in sample (Table 6.1, pp.14-15) `2604.19604v6`.
7. Option-implied rates carry a year-end toll: an unannualized 2.869-3.199 bp discontinuity in the option funding basis when maturity first crosses December 31, behaving as a fixed price wedge scaling about kappa/tau, roughly 10 annualized bp at three months `2609.20224v1`.
8. The fourth-decimal fit precision of the option-implied discount factor (median 0.129 ppm SPX) is not economic purity - the paper's own framing - because the wedge survives the screens `2609.20224v1`.
9. After a negative return jump the S&P 500 option market keeps repricing: ATM implied volatility adds 0.136 in the 5-minute window and 0.218 over 60 minutes, while a positive jump's post-movement vanishes within an hour and out-of-the-money calls adjust immediately (Table 4, p.16) `1810.12200v1`.
10. Out-of-the-money calls and straddles carry negative risk premiums that require unspanned "dark matter" - local time plus jumps crossing the strike - so a negative call premium is possible only if that premium is negative (Theorem 1, p.10); weekly ATM straddles return about -10% unconditionally, negative in 14 of 15 estimates `2303.16371v1`.
11. Whole-chain reads must be allowed to refuse: superhedging produces a piecewise-linear CALL function that tolerates bid-ask spreads and shorting limits, and on a simulated smile 34.74% of quoted prices are inefficient on average, up to 56% (Table 1, p.14) `1502.03978v1`.
12. A closed-form option price with an uncalibrated free parameter is not a measurement: the econophysics review multiplies Black-Scholes by a time-decaying correction carrying an unfitted constant, with no calibration procedure anywhere (Eqs. 3-4, p.2) `2301.11078v1`.

## 1. Implied volatility is a variance quantile, not a root-solve

The Black-Scholes formula is explicit from volatility to price, and market practice has always needed the inverse; the paper shows the inverse is a distributional transform rather than an iteration. In normalized coordinates - call price over discounted forward, log-moneyness, total implied variance - the normalized call price is a probability on the variance scale, and implied variance is a quantile of a generalized inverse Gaussian law, w = F_GIG^-1(c; 1/2, 1/4, k^2) for k > 0 (Eq. 11, p.5) `2604.24480v4`. Equivalently the call price equals the probability that the variance budget Z_k, a GIG(1/2, 1/4, k^2) variable, is at least the realized quadratic path cost, c = P(Z_k <= w) (Eq. 15, p.7). Because volatility is increasing in variance, implied volatility is itself a quantile - a square-root projection of that strike-dependent variance quantile (Eq. 12, p.6). The practical content for this repo is the coordinate, not the special function: `tradingagents/strategies/options_math.py::implied_vol_and_greeks` inverts by bracketed search on price, and `black_vol_surface` already reads a term/vol in variance space, so "variance is the natural coordinate" is a documentation and cross-check statement rather than a rewrite. The evidence is simulation-only - 100,000 randomized cases plus a 246-point tail grid, no market data - and the reported speed advantage (about 59.49 ns vs 180.03 ns per implied-volatility evaluation) is on compiled C++ on one CPU, so it does not transfer directly to a Python calculator. What does transfer is the no-arbitrage statement: the calendar spread is a probability band and the butterfly is a sign condition on the same Greek hierarchy, which is the boundary check a surface fitter should test in variance coordinates rather than in price space.

## 2. Fitting the surface is not fitting the variance term structure

Practitioners calibrate by minimizing Black-Scholes implied-vol errors, and the paper shows this single objective can be badly incomplete. A model whose implied-vol surface matches the market can still imply a variance term structure far from the one the prices contain; at alpha = 1 (pure surface fit) the model-implied VIX term structure has average percentage errors of 10% to 20% of level, while the surface fit itself is comparable to literature standards (pp.24, 35) `2509.08096v1`. The fix is an augmented objective, alpha times the option implied-vol error plus (1 - alpha) times the variance-term-structure error, with alpha in [0, 1] (Eq. 2, p.4) and a practical N(tau) scaling so a 1% variance error at one maturity counts as much as a 1% error across that whole smile (Eq. 39, p.23). The jump caveat is the second result: when the underlying can jump, VIX^2 is only an approximation to the variance-swap rate, and the gap is a third-moment term, VS - VIX^2 about minus lambda times E[J^3]/3 (Eq. 21, p.14), with a log-contract multiplier above 2 under negative skew. This repo carries the two objects that would expose the mismatch - `black_vol_surface` and `variance_swap_strike` and `model_free_implied_variance` in `tradingagents/strategies/options_math.py`, and the VIX term-structure read in `tradingagents/strategies/regime_score.py` - but nothing currently forces them to agree, which is exactly the gap the paper names. A counterweight arrives from `2301.11078v1`: a closed-form American call equal to Black-Scholes times e^{Psi(T-t)(1-r)} (Eq. 3, p.2) with Psi an unfitted constant is a formula without a calibration, so the lesson is not "use more formulas" but "a formula whose free parameter nobody fits is not a measurement".

## 3. Parity holds in price space and leaks carry out of it

This is the slice's headline. Two papers rebuild the same identity from two markets and reach the same shape. The first regresses same-maturity call-put spreads on strike; the slope identifies the option-implied discount factor, and the residual against OIS is a positive carry gap - mean 36.91 bp, median 37.50 bp, 98.4% of observations positive - while the price-space residuals stay in about [-0.1, +0.1] (pp.2, 9) `2604.19604v6`. The paper rules out a fitting artifact: median cell-level R^2 is 0.9999999 for SPX and 0.9999995 for RUT (p.9), and the wedge is explained by a path-risk term proportional to volatility times sqrt(2 tau / pi), scaled by 2/3, plus bid-ask over maturity and the NFCI, for a pooled R^2 of 0.309 (Table 6.1, pp.14-15). Out of sample it is weak - mean R^2 0.049 to 0.075, median up to 0.187, with 2020 a large failure (SPX R^2 -1.634) - so the carry gap is a measured level, not a forecast. The second paper finds a finer structure in the same identity: a positive discontinuity of 2.869-3.199 bp (unannualized) in the option funding basis when maturity first crosses a future December 31, HAC(21) t of 5.30-6.75, behaving as a fixed price wedge scaling about kappa/tau (Tables 5.1-5.3, pp.17-18) `2609.20224v1`; a 2.5 bp fixed charge is about 10 annualized bp at three months and 30 bp at one month (Eq. 25, p.23), and the effect strengthens after a 2018 break (SPX 0.60 to 4.08 bp). Both papers agree that put-call parity identifies the discount rate precisely and does not establish its economic purity. This repo's parity read is `tradingagents/strategies/options_surface.py::parity_violation`, wired through `analysis_tools.py::get_parity_screen`, and its docstring is explicit that it assumes `r = 0, q = 0` and that PV(K) = K over the horizon while publishing last-traded prices. That is the exact assumption both papers falsify: the screen reports only the price-space residual and is blind to a 30-40 bp carry wedge by construction.

## 4. The smile reprices after jumps, and it reprices asymmetrically

Whether an index option market is efficient can be tested by treating a return jump as a news event and watching whether implied volatility moves once or drifts. Using minute-by-minute S&P 500 options over 2006-2010 (1,259 trading days), jumps detected by the Lee-Mykland non-parametric test at 1% on one-minute windows, and 1,226 jumps with 83% before 10:30 (p.8), the paper rejects one-shot adjustment `1810.12200v1`. The total reaction is in the expected direction - positive jumps push ATM implied volatility down, negative jumps up - but there is a documented post-drift excluding the simultaneous reaction: ATM 3-month implied volatility adds 0.136 in the 5-minute window and 0.218 over 60 minutes after a negative jump (Table 4, p.16), rising monotonically 0.136, 0.159, 0.163, 0.191, 0.218 across 5/15/20/30/60 minutes (Table 5, p.25). The asymmetry is the durable finding: negative-jump moves persist or grow over the hour while positive-jump post-movements almost vanish within 60 minutes, out-of-the-money calls show no post-reaction (they converge within the jump window), and the negative-jump betas exceed the positive-jump betas in magnitude across every maturity and moneyness group (pp.13, 15). The repo already reads the smile shape - `surface_shape` (25-delta risk reversal and butterfly) and `term_structure_slope` in `tradingagents/strategies/options_surface.py`, and `pre_event_iv_lift` for the event-time ATM term structure - but it reads one snapshot per run, so a post-jump drift is not observable; the landing is a note that a single-bar smile read cannot see the asymmetry the paper measures, not a new calculator.

## 5. Whole-chain reads must refuse, and the risk premium has a sign

Three papers converge on the honesty of a whole-chain number. Superhedging, which tolerates bid-ask spreads, short-selling limits and margin rather than assuming absence of arbitrage, yields a piecewise-linear CALL function that coincides with the first stage of the Dykstra-style shape-restricted estimator (Eq. 13, p.7) `1502.03978v1`. Smoothness is a deliberate modelling choice on top, and on a Black-Scholes-with-smile simulation (S = 1,365, 25 strikes 1,000 to 1,700, vol falling linearly 40% to 20%, prices 378 down to 0.9) an average 34.74% of prices are inefficient, range 12% to 56%, above 40% in 20% of cases (Table 1, p.14) `1502.03978v1`; the deep-ITM segments are worst, mean 52.12% and max 83.33%. This is the evidence base for a refusal-first design, which the repo already has: `tradingagents/strategies/rnd_recovery.py` recovers a two-component lognormal mixture per expiry and returns `unavailable` with a stated reason and a conditioning spectrum when the covered strikes cannot identify the density, and it takes a minimum of four out-of-the-money prices before it will even try. The risk-premium sign paper supplies what such a recovery should be expected to show: a Tanaka decomposition puts the call risk premium into a dark-matter term (local time plus jumps crossing the strike) plus an upside equity premium, so a negative out-of-the-money call premium requires a negative dark-matter premium, and the straddle premium is negative only if the at-the-money dark-matter premium is (Theorem 1, p.10) `2303.16371v1`. Empirically weekly ATM straddles return about -10% unconditionally, negative in 14 of 15 estimates, and 3% out-of-the-money calls and puts return -59% and -61% over 415 weekly cycles (2011-2018) `2303.16371v1`. The repo's `volatility_risk_premium` leg (IV minus realized vol) and the `rn_skew_proxy` cross-strike slope are the right producers, but neither stores an expected sign; the landing is a documented sign prior so a positive VRP is read as rich options rather than a free edge. The illiquid-proxy paper adds the valuation caveat: an option on an illiquid asset priced by utility indifference with a dynamic proxy hedge plus a static proxy-option hedge is convex in the hedge size, and for extreme moneyness the optimum sits at the edge of the admissible interval rather than at an interior maximum (Fig. 7-8, p.22) `1205.3507v1`, so the value is agent-specific and an interior fit should not be assumed.

## 6. Forecasting implied volatility: what actually moves the number

Two forecasting papers bracket the discipline this repo needs. The genetic-programming study learns an explicit formula for Black-Scholes implied volatility of S&P 500 calls from just three inputs (call over strike, index over strike, time to maturity) over 2003, and shows that dynamic training-subset selection - re-selecting the training subset every g generations and weighting harder subsets more - improves the average MSE from 0.002599 (static, time-series split) and 0.416320 (static, moneyness-maturity split) to 0.002033 (dynamic, combined), with unfitted-observation share falling from 4.27% and 20.29% to 1.77% (Table 5, p.16) `2007.07207v1`. The mechanism is that a static single subset fits only part of the out-of-sample cases, so the win is a generalization win, not a model-class win; the sample is roughly eight months of one index, which is a thin base. The second paper is a negative finding worth recording: a 2010 title "Two stock options at the races: Black-Scholes forecasts" contains no forecasting experiment at all - its "forecasts" are the analytical density of the relative weight of two identically-parameterized Black-Scholes options, logit-normal in the European case with a critical effective maturity alpha_T^c = 1/2 and about 1.63 in the Asian case `1005.1760v2`. The lesson is a title-versus-content caution for the harvest itself: a paper can be filed under forecasting and contain none, and the repo's own forecast discipline - `tradingagents/strategies/calibration.py::diebold_mariano` and `mz_regression` for forecast comparison, and `tradingagents/strategies/forecast_scores.py::rmse, mae, qlike, crps` for scoring rules - is the check that a claimed forecast is scored against a benchmark rather than asserted.

## 7. Instrument scope and the free-parameter trap

The remaining two papers set scope. The Chinese ETF-option study trades intraday short-volatility on the SSE 50ETF and 300ETF options (European, monthly expiry on the last Wednesday), shorting the calls and puts with the most time-value decay and closing intraday, with a GARCH(1,1) volatility forecast mapped to [-1, 1] to scale the position and a 3-day persistence rule that halves size (pp.10, 13, 21) `2403.00474v2`. Its result is a decay: the short-volatility leg delivered excess returns over buy-and-hold before 2018 and those excess returns were annulled and performance deteriorated after 2018 for both underlyings, reported qualitatively with no Sharpe, return or drawdown figure (pp.4, 11, 22). The repo has no execution engine - it is a research layer - so this cannot land as a strategy; it lands as a capacity and decay caution next to `tradingagents/strategies/signal_analysis.py::ic_decay_half_life`, which is the surface that would measure such a decay if the repo ever scored a short-vol rule. The second paper is the free-parameter trap restated: the econophysics review offers closed-form American and Bermudan prices as multiplicative corrections on Black-Scholes, e^{Psi(T-t)(1-r)} times the European price (Eqs. 3-4, p.2) `2301.11078v1`, plus a stochastic-volatility form using a volatility component beta (Eq. 7, p.2), and gives no calibration procedure, no fitted parameter value and no market comparison anywhere - it is a perspective article with equations (1)-(7) confined to pages 1-2. The landing is the repo's existing refusal discipline: a calculator that returns a number with an unfitted free parameter should return `unavailable` with the reason, which is what `tradingagents/strategies/rnd_recovery.py` and the `metric_authority` registry already do.

## Caveats

The evidence does not establish several things. Coverage is narrow and often single-market: the post-jump study is S&P 500 index options over 2006-2010, the dark-matter study is SPX weeklys plus 28-day and 88-day contracts over 1988-2019, the carry-gap study is SPX and RUT over 2016-2025 with weak out-of-sample R^2 (0.049-0.075) and a 2020 failure, and the year-end-toll paper leans on 14 year-end episodes, so none of these is a law. Three of the papers are theory or methodology with simulation-only evidence and no market calibration: the quantile inversion is benchmarked on a randomized grid with no option data, the illiquid-proxy indifference method is validated against finite differences and asymptotic expansions rather than traded prices, and the superhedging estimator is tested on a simulated smile plus limited real quotes. One paper (`2301.11078v1`) is a perspective review and one (`1005.1760v2`) contains no experiment at all. The forecasting study is roughly eight months of one index. The Chinese ETF study is one market pair and reports its decay qualitatively, with no cost-adjusted return series, so it cannot be read as a net-of-cost result. All the carry and parity numbers are unannualized or annualized depending on the paper and are not cost-adjusted in this brief; overlapping windows and non-synchronous quotes remain in every empirical sample, and the repo's own chain reads use last traded prices and a zero-rate assumption, so any comparison between a paper number and a repo number must first reconcile those two bases.
