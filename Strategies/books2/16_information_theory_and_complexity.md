# Information theory, entropy and complexity: an entropy or complexity feature earns its place only when it carries a null and a coverage window, and the slice supplies both.

Date: 2026-10-07. Revision 1.

This brief covers the arXiv records that treat information theory, entropy and complexity as measurement instruments for markets rather than as metaphors - entropy as an inequality/decomposition device, entropy and fractal-dimension estimators applied to price series, and, the part that decides usability, how those estimators behave on short, noisy, non-stationary samples. The slice holds 61 records spanning 2007-2026, 11 with a journal reference. Forty candidates were screened and ten were read from the PDFs, with page anchors. It is written for the research and analysis layer of this repository: the deterministic calculators under `tradingagents/strategies/`, the score engines, the `coverage_window`/`data_quality`/`metric_authority` measurement surfaces, and the `complexity.py` entropy features that already reach the agent tools. Every take-away lands on one of those surfaces; where a finding is only actionable in an execution layer, the row says so.

## What this covers

The arc runs from entropy as a market-state and inequality measure (`2205.00104v1`, `1301.5504v1`) through entropy and roughness estimators fitted to price series (`1206.1007v1`, `2310.18903v3`) to the two questions that decide whether any of it is usable: what null does the statistic have, and how short a window can carry it (`2512.02352v3`, `2512.23596v2`). The single most consequential finding for this repo is that the forward visibility horizon of a path is exactly a first-passage time, its i.i.d. survival law is exactly `Pr[L+ >= k] = 1/k` with infinite mean and variance, and the roughness exponent it estimates is exactly `theta(H) = 1 - H` (`2512.02352v3`, p.4, p.5, p.7). That gives a rank-invariant, parameter-free statistic with a closed-form null - and a measured finite-sample bias that says when not to trust it (p.8).

The consequence is uncomfortable for the current implementation. `tradingagents/strategies/complexity.py` publishes `permutation_entropy`, `approximate_entropy` and `lz_complexity`, and `tradingagents/agents/utils/analysis_tools.py::get_mean_reversion_quality` and `::get_shift_detection` cite them. None of the three carries a null, a sample-size floor tied to an error figure, or a coverage window, and the shift read converts `lz_complexity` into a verdict with a hand-picked constant, `'random/inefficient' if lzc > 0.9 else 'structured'`. The slice says the constant should be an empirical null band and the estimator should report its threshold and tail size (`2512.02352v3`, p.16, p.18).

## The short answer

1. The forward visibility horizon of a path is exactly its first-passage time, with the exact i.i.d. null `Pr[L+ >= k] = 1/k` and infinite mean and variance, so roughness must be read off a tail exponent, never a moment `2512.02352v3` (p.4, p.5).
2. That tail exponent satisfies `theta(H) = 1 - H` for fractional Brownian motion and recovers it within one cross-replicate standard deviation for `H <= 0.2` at `T = 2^16`; the positive finite-size bias is `+0.075` at `H = 0.5` and `+0.323` at `H = 0.9` `2512.02352v3` (p.7, p.8).
3. The empirical i.i.d. null for the same estimator is `1.166` with a 2.5% quantile of `1.017` at window `W = 1024` `2512.02352v3` (p.18), so the fixed `lzc > 0.9` verdict in `get_shift_detection` is a hand threshold standing where a calibrated null belongs.
4. The statistic separates rough Bergomi from classical Heston by `0.3355` (combined Monte-Carlo SE `0.00024`) but from FIGARCH by only `0.0580`, too small to falsify long-memory volatility on less than about ten years of daily data `2512.02352v3` (p.13).
5. A DFA scaling range is a fitted linear function of series length and fit quality, `lambda = (a u + a0) L + b` with `u = 1 - R2`, so the folk rule `tau_max ~ L/4` is a choice with a quantified rejection rate, not a law `1206.1007v1` (p.3, p.5).
6. The scaling range grows only 3-10% for a `0.1` change in the Hurst exponent, so a short window cannot resolve `H` precisely regardless of the estimator `1206.1007v1` (p.7).
7. The cross-sectional intrinsic entropy is at least an order of magnitude more variable than index volatility estimators, and index beta against it is 50-90% lower `2205.00104v1` (p.15, p.20).
8. Efficiency has a clean information-theoretic definition, `Eff(X|Y) = H(X|Y) / H(X)`, which equals normalized Kelly growth, and inefficiency has two separable sources: predictability and wrong pricing `1812.02371v1` (p.6, p.9, p.11).
9. A coin predictable to nearly 90% is still about 50% efficient, and a 10% departure from unpredictability moves efficiency by less than 3% - but that 3% is a 3% mean return per toss with no bankruptcy risk `1812.02371v1` (p.14).
10. Model complexity and training-window length must be chosen jointly under non-stationarity; the prediction-error bound adds a total-variation drift term and a matching lower bound makes an error of order `V` unavoidable `2512.23596v2` (p.15, p.18); the adaptive procedure lifts out-of-sample R2 to `0.049` (+14%) and monthly Sharpe from `0.30` to `0.33` `2512.23596v2` (p.3, p.4).
11. Mutual information detects nonlinear dependence that Pearson correlation misses: at `alpha = 0.01` `40.77%` and `52.99%` of correlations are insignificant while every MI estimate is significant, and the MI minimum spanning tree shows a power-law weighted-degree tail (exponent `2.39`) where the correlation tree is log-normal (`4.19`) `1711.06185v3` (p.9, p.14).
12. Correlation-graph sparsity is a crisis signal: over the top 1% of correlations the edge count falls `455 -> 294` during the crisis while the share of isolated nodes rises `39.52% -> 59.97%`, even though whole-market average correlation rises `2308.02914v2` (p.5).

## 1. A rank-invariant roughness statistic with an exact null

`2512.02352v3` is the anchor of the slice. It studies the horizontal visibility graph of a path and isolates the forward visibility horizon `L+(t)`, the largest `k` such that `t+k` is HVG-visible from `t` and `x_{t+k} >= x_t` (p.4). The load-bearing result is Lemma 1: for a path with no ties, each uncensored horizon equals the classical first-passage time `tau+(t) = inf{k >= 1 : x_{t+k} >= x_t}` (p.4). Terminal non-crossings are right-censored, which is a real finite-sample issue rather than a technicality. With that identity, Proposition 2 gives the exact i.i.d. survival law `Pr[L+ >= k] = 1/k`, Renyi's record statistic; the mean and variance both diverge (p.5). This is why the paper estimates a power-law tail exponent `theta` rather than a mean, and why any mean-horizon estimator is ill-posed.

Corollary 3 combines the identity with discrete-grid persistence theory for fractional Brownian motion to predict `theta(H) = 1 - H` (p.7). The numerical validation uses `N = 10,000` fBm paths of length `T = 2^16` sampled by Davies-Harte, with a Hill maximum-likelihood fit, a Clauset-Shalizi-Newman threshold and a stationary block bootstrap of mean block length `ceil(T^(1/3))` and `B = 200` resamples (p.6, p.7). Table 1 reports the recovery and, importantly, the bias: at `H = 0.1`, `theta-hat = 0.900` with `sigma = 0.013` and bias `-0.0003`; at `H = 0.2`, `0.810` with bias `+0.010`; at `H = 0.5`, `0.575` with bias `+0.075`; at `H = 0.9`, `0.423` with bias `+0.323` (p.8). The bias decays with `T` (for `H = 0.1` it is `+0.078` at `T = 2^12` and `-0.010` at `T = 2^18`; for `H = 0.5` it is `+0.200` at `T = 2^12` and `+0.046` at `T = 2^18`), but for `H >= 0.7` no length below `T = 2^18` brings it under `0.1` (p.16, p.17). Control processes confirm the null is right in practice: i.i.d. uniform gives `1.021` and i.i.d. Gaussian `1.024` against the asymptotic `1.00`, and Brownian motion gives `0.569` against the fBm `H = 0.5` value `0.50` (p.9).

Two robustness results matter for a research stack. Additive Gaussian noise pushes the estimate toward the i.i.d. null (`0.984` at relative noise `sigma = 1.0`, below `0.02` bias for `sigma <= 0.3 std(x)`), and random thinning up to 50% leaves the estimate unchanged within Monte-Carlo SE (p.17). Rank-invariance is why geometric Brownian motion gives the same answer as arithmetic Brownian motion (p.9). Applied to daily FRED VIX from 2000-01-03 to 2026-04-30 (`n = 6651`), with window `W = 1024` and step 126 days, the 45 rolling windows average `theta-hat = 0.910` with standard deviation `0.19` and range `[0.723, 1.506]`; 35 of 45 windows fall below the single-window 2.5% null quantile of `1.017`, against an overlapping-window i.i.d. null with mean `1.160` and standard deviation `0.026`, a Monte-Carlo tail probability of about `0.001` (p.13, p.14, p.15).

The honest reading for this repo is methodological. The statistic is not additional alpha; it is a demonstration that a complexity number is interpretable only beside its null, its threshold and its tail size - exactly the triple `tradingagents/strategies/complexity.py` does not currently emit.

## 2. How many points an entropy or Hurst estimate needs

`1206.1007v1` attacks the question every DFA user faces: over which box sizes is the power law `F2(tau) ~ tau^(2H)` actually linear? It fixes `tau_min = 8` because shorter boxes manufacture artificial autocorrelation, and notes the folk rule `tau_max ~ L/4` (p.3). It simulates ensembles of `5 x 10^4` series with lengths `5 x 10^2 <= L <= 2 x 10^4` for the uncorrelated case and up to `L = 10^4` for the correlated case, generated by Fourier filtering for `0.5 < H < 0.9` (p.4, p.5). The scaling range `lambda` is a clean linear function of both `L` and `u = 1 - R2`: `lambda = (a u + a0) L + b` (p.5). Table 1 gives the fitted coefficients at 97.5% confidence: at `H = 0.5`, `a = 6.02`, `a0 = 0.0034`, `b = -92`; at `H = 0.8`, `a = 6.88`, `a0 = 0.0136`, `b = -100`, with mean absolute error 1.5-2.5% (p.6). The unified form `lambda = ((alpha H + beta) u + alpha0) L + gamma` uses four free parameters, fitted as `alpha = 3.40`, `beta = 4.16`, `alpha0 = 0.0097`, `gamma = -96` at 97.5% confidence (p.7).

The practical content is in two numbers. First, a negative `lambda` from the fitted formula means no scaling range exists at that confidence level and series length, which the paper states explicitly (p.5). Second, the scaling range increases by only 3-10% for every `delta H = 0.1` (p.7, Eq. 9). A Hurst estimate on a few hundred points therefore carries irreducible resolution error, which is the same lesson `2512.02352v3` reaches from the other direction: short windows and smooth paths are the hard regime.

The companion numbers are the empirical null distribution in Table B.5 of `2512.02352v3`: the mean of the Hill-CSN estimator on i.i.d. uniform replicates is `1.211` at `W = 512`, `1.166` at `1024`, `1.136` at `2048`, decaying only logarithmically slowly toward the asymptotic `1.00`, with 2.5% quantiles of `1.010` and `1.017` at the two shortest windows (p.18). A rolling complexity estimator at a window below about a thousand points should be compared against these, not against the asymptotic value.

## 3. Entropy as a cross-sectional market-state measure

`2205.00104v1` defines the cross-sectional intrinsic entropy from daily OHLC and volume for every symbol traded on the NYSE and NASDAQ, weighting each symbol's traded value into a market-wide entropy (p.8). The data cover 5295 reference points from 2001-01-01 to 21 January 2022, with a single NYSE file holding 3562 symbols (p.4, p.9). The headline claim is that the variance of the cross-sectional entropy is consistently at least one order of magnitude above the variance of index volatility estimators, for every time interval and rolling window tested (Table 4, p.15). Index beta against it is 50-90% lower than the market's own volatility risk, and lower on shorter intervals: over 90% lower at 30 days and over 75% at 60 days (p.20). Correlation between the entropy measure and index estimators rises with the window, from `0.445` for close-to-close at 30 days with a 5-day moving average up to `0.895` at 5295 days with a 30-day average (Table 5, p.17). One structural caveat the paper states plainly: the number of traded symbols is itself non-stationary, from about 1000 in 2001 to over 3500 in 2022, and is treated as the number of microstates (p.3, p.5).

The relevant caution for this repo is already encoded in `tradingagents/strategies/factor_dispersion.py`: a dispersion measure is only comparable when its scale is declared, which is why `factor_score_dispersion` names `FACTOR_SCORE_MIN`, `FACTOR_SCORE_MAX` and `MAXIMUM_POSSIBLE_STD` rather than assuming them. The cross-sectional entropy is the same kind of object - its variance is larger and its level non-stationary - so any new market-state entropy leg must ship with a declared universe and a declared microstate count, which is what `tradingagents/strategies/coverage_window.py::universe_label` records for panel statistics.

## 4. Efficiency as a conditional-entropy ratio, and what it would replace

`1812.02371v1` replaces the binary efficient-market debate with a measure. Efficiency of system `X` relative to information `Y` is `Eff(X|Y) = H(X|Y) / H(X)` (p.6), where `H` is Shannon entropy in bits. A fully efficient system has `H(X|Y) = H(X)` and mutual information `M(X, Y*) = 0`; a fully inefficient one has `H(X|Y) = 0` and `M(X, Y*) = H(X)` (p.8). The same expression comes from Kelly's optimal-growth result, where maximal capital growth is exactly the mutual information `G_max(X|Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y)` (p.9), which is what makes it more than a restatement of Fama. The extension to unfair quotes splits inefficiency into two nearly independent sources: predictability, which lowers `H(X|Y)`, and wrong pricing, which raises the denominator `H(q)` (p.11).

The coin-tossing illustration gives the shape of the predictability effect and is the part that matters for a signal-research surface: efficiency is still around 50% when the outcome can be predicted with nearly 90% accuracy, a 10% departure from unpredictability moves efficiency by less than 3%, and that 3% is itself a 3% mean return per toss with no bankruptcy risk (p.14). Mispricing is nearly as forgiving: efficiency is around 60% even when the mispricing is about 90%, and around 30% even at 99% (p.15). The message is that a naive accuracy number overstates inefficiency, while the entropy ratio is directly comparable to achievable growth.

This is the paper in the slice that most directly targets the measurement layer. It says that `tradingagents/strategies/signal_analysis.py::rank_ic` and `tradingagents/strategies/alpha_health.py::ic_label` measure predictability, not efficiency, and that the two are not on the same scale - a rank IC must be read beside the growth it implies before it is called strong. The two-source split also names the difference between a low `rank_information_coefficient` and a high conditional entropy of the score-return relationship, which `1812.02371v1` argues are separate causes of apparent inefficiency (p.11).

## 5. Complexity must be chosen with the training window

`2512.23596v2` formalises why more data does not always help. Under non-stationarity the joint distribution of predictors and returns drifts, so a longer window imports stale regimes. The prediction-error bound (Theorem 3.1) decomposes excess risk into model misspecification, local Rademacher statistical uncertainty, a `log(1/delta)/n` term, and `M^2 max_{t-k <= tau <= t-1} TV(P_tau, P_t)` - the largest total-variation shift between any training period and the prediction date (p.15, p.16). A matching lower bound shows the drift term is not a proof artifact: over a class of environments with drift magnitude `V`, an error of order `V` is unavoidable (p.18). In a stylised example the optimal window scales as `k* ~ eta^(-1/2)` for a linear class and `k* ~ eta^(-3/5)` for a kernel class, with `eta` the drift severity; the kernel class wins when `eta = O(gamma^5)` and the linear class wins when `eta` is much larger than `gamma^5` (p.18).

Empirically, on 17 US industry portfolios from 1990-2016, an adaptive joint selection lifts out-of-sample R2 to `0.049`, a 14% improvement over the best fixed-window benchmark, and monthly mean-variance Sharpe from `0.30` to `0.33` without higher turnover (p.3, p.4). The gains concentrate in the three NBER recessions: `0.027` in 1990, `0.125` in 2001, and `0.041` in 2008 (p.4). The empirical illustration uses a 64-month recent window against all-history training (p.10). The research-layer consequence is that every fixed lookback in the repo - the `Hurst` window in `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py`, the memory profile in `long_memory.py`, the change-point reads in `regime.py` - is one point on the complexity/window tradeoff, and the drift term is currently unmeasured. `tradingagents/strategies/coverage_window.py` is the natural place to record it, because it already names the window a panel statistic ran over.

## 6. Mutual information against Pearson in the dependence matrix

`1711.06185v3` compares minimum spanning trees built from mutual information against trees built from Pearson correlation, on Brazilian equity returns sampled every 15 minutes across two 2015-2016 periods: 3888 returns in the Rousseff period (index `-42%`) and 3969 in the transition period (`+50%`) (p.8). Mutual information is estimated non-parametrically by Gaussian kernel density estimation and converted to a comparable coefficient `l_ij = sqrt(1 - exp(-2 I_ij))` (p.5). Significance is checked at `alpha = 0.01`: `40.77%` of correlations are insignificant in the first period and `52.99%` in the second, while all MI estimates are significant (p.9). The network verdicts differ: the MI trees have smaller mean distance and, in the more turbulent second period, power-law weighted-degree tails with fitted exponent `2.39` against `4.19` for the correlation tree (p.14, Table 2). The robustness coefficient rises 27% in that period and the mean MST distance falls 18% (p.15). A portfolio of the 20 most central assets under the MI tree would have returned 40% more than the correlation-based analogue in the transition period (p.14). The caveat is sample size: the MI estimates are averaged over each period and estimated by kernel density on a few thousand points.

`2310.18903v3` is the same visibility-graph family applied to a real panel: WTI, Brent and Shanghai (SC) crude oil futures prices are converted into visibility graphs across daily, 5-minute, 15-minute and 30-minute frequencies from 2018-03-26 to 2023-07-20 (p.3). Frequencies are chosen at 5/15/30 minutes specifically to suppress noise (p.3). Degree distributions show power-law tails with exponent `alpha` fluctuating between 2 and 3.5; in the pre-COVID sub-sample all three markets sit at `alpha = 3.5`, and over the whole sample all exponents drop below 3 (p.9). Global clustering coefficients hover around `0.7` and peak at `0.7442` (p.12, Table 2), and the graphs satisfy the small-world relation `L(N) ~ ln N` (p.11). Assortativity is where the markets separate: during the pandemic sub-sample the SC daily assortativity coefficients fall to `0.0205` and `0.0611`, essentially zero, while WTI and Brent show their lowest values after the Russia-Ukraine conflict (p.14). The lesson for a backtest is that graph topology is a noisy but regime-sensitive summary, and the sampling frequency changes it materially.

The research-layer read is direct. `tradingagents/strategies/cross_section.py::residualize_returns` and `::industry_neutral_z` are Pearson-linear; `tradingagents/strategies/breadth_depth.py::average_pairwise_correlation` and `tradingagents/strategies/triadic_stress.py::triadic_stress` are built on linear correlation. The MI result says a correlation of zero is not independence, and that a dependence screen should carry an MI leg wherever the linear screen reads insignificant.

## 7. Flow entropy, hierarchy, and the two theory limits

`1301.5504v1` models cash flows between `N` agents as probabilities that a random currency unit sits in a given flow, `H = -sum p log2 p` (p.2). Its structural result is the subdivision identity `H = H_g + sum_j p_j H_j`, which recursively yields inequality measures at every level of aggregation (p.2, Eq. 5); grouping agents into human, corporate and government sectors recovers a measure related to the Theil index as a special case (p.4). The balance constraint is the interesting part: in a steady state, `sum_j p_j (H_j - H^j) = 0`, where `H_j` is the entropy of an agent's inflows and `H^j` of its outflows (p.4, Eq. 28). No economy can have every agent more certain about inflows than outflows; entropy-increasing and entropy-decreasing agents must coexist. The three-agent worked example makes it concrete with inflow entropies `0.8617, 0.8048, 0.5275` and outflow entropies `0.4690, 0.8813, 0.8813` (p.7). For a trading book this is a decomposition of fill and payment concentration, and a reminder that a concentration measure is only meaningful relative to the flows it is drawn from - which is what `tradingagents/strategies/portfolio.py::weight_entropy` and `::weight_hhi` report at one level only.

`2308.02914v2` builds a correlation graph over 802 global stocks with 1847 observations from 2004-10-27 to 2019-03-15, keeps the top 1% of correlations, and scores anomalies with an autoencoder's Tsallis entropy (p.4). The structural finding is a sparsity paradox: in the top 1% of correlations the number of edges falls from 455 before the crisis to 294 during it, and the share of nodes with no edges rises from `39.52%` to `59.97%`, even though average correlation across all assets rises (p.5). Clustering falls from `0.30` to `0.26` and recovers to `0.37`, and the anomaly counts differ across periods at p-values from `1.57e-4` to `6.65e-10` (p.5, p.6). The paper states its own limit: there is no ground truth for the detected anomalies (p.6), so this is a monitor to add beside `tradingagents/strategies/breadth_depth.py::concentration_hhi`, not a signal to trade.

`2309.02447v2` is a theory paper with no data, included for its falsifiable claim. It derives market-based averages and volatilities of price and return from the statistical moments of trade values and volumes, so that price volatility depends on the second moments of trade value and volume and on their correlation (p.6). Because volatility forecasts therefore require models of second-order economic variables - sums of squares of trade values - and no such second-order macroeconomic theory exists, the paper argues that price and return probability forecasts are capped near Gaussian accuracy (p.3, p.6). This is a limitation statement, not a result to act on, and it should be recorded as such in the forecast surface: it says a documented vol-of-vol uncertainty is irreducible at present, not a number to invent.

## Caveats

Half of this slice is not about tradable instruments. `1301.5504v1`, `1812.02371v1`, `2309.02447v2` and the network papers (`1711.06185v3`, `2308.02914v2`) are about economies, games, firms and cross-sections, not about execution or price series, and no paper here measures transfer entropy or directed information flow on order flow - the closest is undirected mutual information in `1711.06185v3`. `1206.1007v1` and `2512.02352v3` are simulation studies, so their bias and null tables are synthetic and their empirical checks are single-series (VIX for the latter). `2310.18903v3` is three futures markets over one five-year window and is descriptive, with no out-of-sample test. `2205.00104v1` is US equities over one 21-year window with a changing symbol count, which the paper itself flags as a non-stationary microstate count. `1711.06185v3` is one emerging market over two consecutive periods, and its MI estimates are averaged over each period. `2308.02914v2` has no ground truth for its anomalies. `2309.02447v2` is a position paper with no data, `1812.02371v1` is illustrated only on a coin-tossing game, and `1301.5504v1` is purely formal with no empirical calibration, no credit and no time dimension. `2512.23596v2` proves its bound under an i.i.d.-within-batch assumption and tests on 17 US industry portfolios. No measure here carries cost or capacity, so none of the entropy numbers should be read as a return; they are filters and state variables, and any that reaches a score must carry the coverage discipline the score kernel already enforces.

## Papers read in depth

- `2512.02352v3` - First-passage horizons in horizontal visibility graphs: a rank-invariant estimator of path roughness for rough volatility models (2025). Provides the exact i.i.d. null, `theta(H) = 1 - H`, and a measured finite-size bias; the rough-Bergomi result rests on an unproven persistence hypothesis and the rough-versus-FIGARCH separation is small.
- `1206.1007v1` - On the scaling ranges of detrended fluctuation analysis for long-memory correlated short series of data (2012). Gives a fitted formula for the usable DFA scaling range as a function of length, fit quality and Hurst exponent; entirely simulation-based and calibrated only up to `L = 2 x 10^4`.
- `2205.00104v1` - The Cross-Sectional Intrinsic Entropy. A Comprehensive Stock Market Volatility Estimator (2022). Defines a market-wide OHLCV entropy and shows it is far more variable than index volatility; single market family, and the non-stationary symbol count complicates comparability.
- `1812.02371v1` - Quantification of market efficiency based on informational-entropy (2018). Derives `Eff = H(X|Y)/H(X)` from both Fama and Kelly and splits inefficiency into two sources; illustrated only on a coin-tossing game, with no empirical market application.
- `2512.23596v2` - The Nonstationarity-Complexity Tradeoff in Return Prediction (2025). Proves a prediction-error bound with an explicit drift term and a joint selection procedure; the theory assumes i.i.d. batches and the empirical test is 17 US industry portfolios.
- `1711.06185v3` - Nonlinear dependencies on Brazilian equity network from mutual information minimum spanning trees (2019). Compares MI and correlation spanning trees and finds MI exposes power-law degree tails; two short adjacent periods in one emerging market.
- `1301.5504v1` - Cash Flow Entropy (2013). Builds a hierarchical flow-entropy measure recovering Theil and derives the entropy-balance constraint; purely formal, with no empirical calibration and no credit or time dimension.
- `2308.02914v2` - Anomaly Detection in Global Financial Markets with Graph Neural Networks and Nonextensive Entropy (2023). Uses Tsallis entropy as an autoencoder anomaly score and documents crisis-driven graph sparsification; no ground-truth labels for the anomalies.
- `2309.02447v2` - Economic Complexity Limits Accuracy of Price Probability Predictions by Gaussian Distributions (2023). Argues volatility forecasts need second-order trade-moment models and are therefore capped near Gaussian accuracy; a position paper with no data and no test.
- `2310.18903v3` - Visibility graph analysis of crude oil futures markets: Insights from the COVID-19 pandemic and Russia-Ukraine conflict (2023). Shows power-law degree tails, high clustering and regime-dependent assortativity in three oil futures; descriptive, with no out-of-sample test and one sample window.

## Where to next in the corpus

- `0909.3890v1` - The Building Blocks of Economic Complexity. The canonical fitness-complexity construction; a lead for turning the network diagnostics here into a ranking rather than a topology summary.
- `1705.02154v2` - Leontief Meets Shannon - Measuring the Complexity of the Economic System. A direct bridge between input-output accounting and information-theoretic complexity, relevant if flow entropy is applied to cash flows rather than returns.
- `1607.02481v4` - Inferring monopartite projections of bipartite networks: an entropy-based approach. A method for validating projected networks against an entropy null, which would harden the correlation and MI graphs used above.
- `2604.17166v2` - The Virtue of Sparsity in Complexity. A 2026 counterpoint to complexity-as-virtue; a lead for the joint complexity/window question in `2512.23596v2`.
- `2407.00022v1` - Entropy and Economics. A survey lead for the definitions used loosely across this slice.
