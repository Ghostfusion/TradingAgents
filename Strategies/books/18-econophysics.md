# Econophysics, Scaling & Agent-Based Models

`book 18/19` · slug `econophysics` · 985 papers in the corpus · 60 rated high-relevance by the sweep

## 0. Scope

This category covers the physics-style study of markets: power-law tail exponents, scaling and long-memory
laws, multifractal analysis (MF-DFA, MF-DMA, wavelet leaders), self-exciting Hawkes point processes for
order arrival and volatility clustering, and agent-based market models that try to reproduce stylised facts
from microstructure. It matters to this repo in a narrow, disciplined way: the tail/risk-scaling and
long-memory results bear directly on `book_risk.py`, `tail_risk.py`, `long_memory.py`, `mean_reversion.py`
and `volatility_models.py`; the Hawkes and ABM work is mostly diagnostic — it tells you *why* the stylised
facts the repo already measures exist, and warns against narrating effects that are not actually there.
The bulk of the category is descriptive and not directly tradeable, so this book is deliberately sceptical.

## 2. Deep reads

### arXiv 1201.4786v1 — On Hurst exponent estimation under heavy-tailed distributions (2012)
- Question: how do finite-sample properties of Hurst estimators change when the process is heavy-tailed
  (stable distribution, stability exponent α from 1.1 to 2.0) rather than Gaussian?
- Method: Monte Carlo over four estimators — R/S, MF-DFA, DMA, and the generalized Hurst exponent (GHE) —
  for series lengths 512–65,536, then an intraday time-dependent Hurst study of S&P 500.
- Finding: **R/S and GHE are robust to heavy tails; MF-DFA and DMA are not.** GHE has the lowest bias and
  variance across all α and all sample sizes. Most published practice — comparing an estimate to 0.5 using
  Gaussian simulation confidence bands — is invalid for financial returns.
- Limitation: independent (uncorrelated) series only; it isolates the tail effect, not the joint
  tail+correlation effect.

### arXiv 1307.2014v2 — On the multifractal effects generated by monofractal signals (2013)
- Question: how much "multifractality" does MF-DFA manufacture from a genuinely monofractal series?
- Method: analytic + simulation study of the apparent spread Δh of generalized Hurst exponents and width
  Δα of the multifractal spectrum as a function of series length and autocorrelation exponent γ.
- Finding: finite length alone produces a non-zero Δh/Δα for monofractal signals; the false signal is
  **linear in γ and decays as a power law with series length**. The authors give threshold formulas below
  which a non-zero Δα/Δh must be treated as "apparent", not real.
- Limitation: thresholds are semi-analytical and fitted for their synthetic generators; applying them to a
  given real series requires re-deriving the background.

### arXiv 2211.00728v2 — Genuine multifractality in time series is due to temporal correlations (2023)
- Question: is multifractality caused by fat tails or by temporal correlations?
- Method: MFDFA asymptotics for uncorrelated series in the Gaussian (and Lévy) basin of attraction, plus
  simulations.
- Finding: for uncorrelated series the multifractal signature **vanishes asymptotically** as length grows
  for positive moments; only the monofractal (Gaussian) or bifractal (Lévy) case survives. Genuine
  multifractality requires long-range temporal correlations; broad tails can only *widen* the spectrum when
  correlations are present. The question is therefore ill-posed.
- Limitation: asymptotic argument — finite samples still show the (spurious) width of `1307.2014`.

### arXiv 1005.0877v2 — Detrending moving average algorithm for multifractals (2010)
- Question: can the detrending-moving-average (DMA) family replace polynomial detrending in multifractal
  analysis, and which window position is best?
- Method: develop MFDMA for 1-D and 2-D measures; test backward (θ=0), centered (θ=0.5), forward (θ=1)
  against analytically-solvable synthetic multifractals; apply to Shanghai Composite.
- Finding: **backward MFDMA (θ=0) has the lowest error bars and outperforms MF-DFA**, while the centered
  variant is the worst. Scaling exponent τ(q) and spectrum f(α) match theory.
- Limitation: synthetic measures with known analytic spectra; real-series behaviour depends on the chosen
  scaling range.

### arXiv physics/0601174v2 — Long-term memory and volatility clustering (2006)
- Question: is the long memory detected by DFA in returns or in volatility, and can it be attributed to a
  known mechanism?
- Method: DFA on daily and high-frequency indices/FX; then re-run after AR(1) filtering (short memory) and
  GARCH(1,1) filtering (volatility clustering).
- Finding: **no significant long memory in returns; strong long memory in volatility.** AR(1) filtering
  leaves the volatility memory unchanged; GARCH(1,1) filtering removes or greatly diminishes it — so the
  volatility DFA memory is an expression of volatility clustering, not an independent slow process.
- Limitation: DFA is itself finite-sample-biased (see `1307.2014`).

### arXiv 1410.3394v1 — Volatility is rough (2014)
- Question: how smooth is the log-volatility process?
- Method: estimate Hurst exponent of log-volatility from high-frequency data; fit a rough fractional
  stochastic volatility (RFSV) model; study the tiny-H regime.
- Finding: log-volatility behaves as fractional Brownian motion with **H ≈ 0.1 at all reasonable scales**
  (H < 0.5 = "rough"). The RFSV model improves realized-volatility forecasts, and — importantly — **classical
  persistence tests conclude "long memory" on data generated by a rough (non-long-memory) process**, which
  explains why volatility long memory became a stylized fact.
- Limitation: H ≈ 0.1 is so small that measurement is dominated by the microstructure/estimation filter.

### arXiv 2203.13820v3 — Rough volatility: fact or artefact? (2023)
- Question: is the roughness seen in realized volatility a property of spot volatility or of the estimator?
- Method: non-parametric normalized p-th variation roughness estimator; applied to simulated diffusive and
  fractional volatility models and to AAPL/SP500 high-frequency data.
- Finding: **realized volatility always looks rough (H < 0.5) even when instantaneous volatility is
  Brownian**, because the estimation error is rough. The industry-standard "rough volatility" reading is at
  least partly an estimation artefact; instantaneous-volatility roughness is not identifiable from realized
  volatility alone.
- Limitation: does not settle whether spot volatility is rough or not — only that realized-vol roughness is
  not evidence for it.

### arXiv 1302.1405v2 — Critical reflexivity in financial markets: a Hawkes process analysis (2013)
- Question: has market endogeneity (reflexivity) grown, as an earlier study claimed?
- Method: fit a self-exciting Hawkes process to E-Mini S&P 500 mid-price changes; test power-law vs
  exponential kernels across 1998–2011.
- Finding: the kernel is **power-law with two regimes** — exponent ≈ −1.15 below ~10³ s crossing to ≈ −1.45
  in [10³, 10⁶] s — and it **integrates to unity (branching ratio n ≈ 1) in every period**. Markets sit
  near criticality; the apparent "increased reflexivity" of the earlier study came from fitting an
  exponential kernel on 30-minute windows and thus seeing only short-term reflexivity. The correlation scale
  has shortened over time, not the total endogeneity.
- Limitation: one instrument (E-Mini S&P); criticality (n=1) is the edge of the stationary region, so fits
  are delicate.

### arXiv 1201.3572v2 — Quantifying reflexivity in financial markets (2012)
- Question: what fraction of price changes is endogenous (feedback) vs exogenous (news)?
- Method: calibrate self-excited conditional Poisson Hawkes to E-Mini S&P 500, 1998–2010; define
  endogeneity as the branching ratio.
- Finding: the exogenous share of price changes fell from ~70% in 1998 to **under 30% since 2007** — the
  measure FS proposed as a distance-to-criticality early-warning tool (and which detected precursors of the
  2010 flash crash on 10-minute windows). This is the claim `1302.1405` disputes by kernel choice; together
  they fix the honest reading as "short-scale endogeneity rose, aggregate branching ratio stays ≈ 1".
- Limitation: exponential-kernel fit on short windows; disputes with `1302.1405` are exactly about that.

### arXiv 2202.01043v2 — 2T-POT Hawkes model for left- and right-tail conditional quantile forecasts (2022)
- Question: are extreme losses and gains conditionally asymmetric, and does a Hawkes tail model forecast
  better than GARCH-EVT?
- Method: two-tailed peaks-over-threshold Hawkes (asymmetric self/cross-excitation) fitted to daily
  log-returns of six large-cap indices, over thresholds 1.25%–25%, out-of-sample 2015–2022.
- Finding: consistent **temporal leverage effect** — losses contribute ~2.2× as much to the shared
  conditional intensity and decay ~4.6× faster than gains. The 2T-POT Hawkes beats GARCH-EVT for
  (mirrored) VaR/ES at the 5% coverage level and below.
- Limitation: daily index data; the bulk (intra-threshold) distribution is appended subordinately and the
  model is fit-heavy.

### arXiv 2209.07092v2 — Measuring tail risks: most probable maximum risk (2022)
- Question: a tail measure that needs no confidence level and extrapolates short-term to long-term risk.
- Method: MPMR = mode of the block maximum of size n; derived analytically for normal, exponential,
  Pareto and Student-t; TI estimator from the slope of log MPMR vs log n.
- Finding: for Pareto-type sizes **MPMR(n) ∝ n^η with η = 1/ξ** (ξ = tail index), so **ξ̂ = 1/η̂** is a
  robust, low-bias tail-index estimator; the sub-sampling scheme uses n up to N(1−1/e) and M=600 blocks.
  Compared favourably to Hill, least-squares, percentile and MLE estimators under Student-t.
- Limitation: i.i.d. block maxima — time clustering (Hawkes) is outside the model.

### arXiv 2312.05655v1 — A novel scaling approach for unbiased adjustment of risk estimators (2023)
- Question: how to scale a risk estimator to longer horizons / more extreme percentiles without the
  square-root-of-time rule.
- Method: a statistical scaling framework with a direct link to backtesting (Kupiec-style), for VaR and ES.
- Finding: risk estimates degrade badly when extrapolated by sqrt-time to long horizons or extreme
  percentiles; a conservative, unbiased scaling exists that can beat sqrt-time and support capital
  allocation. **Depth risk does not scale as sqrt(T).**
- Limitation: needs a reasonably long estimation history to calibrate the scaling.

### arXiv 2312.14903v2 — Scalable agent-based modelling for complex financial market simulations (2023)
- Question: can a bottom-up ABM reproduce market stylised facts without fitting to history?
- Method: heterogeneous agents, parallel decisions, continuous double-auction order-book matching engine,
  multiple simultaneous assets, distributed scale-out; baseline model validated on stylised facts.
- Finding: the simulator reproduces fat tails, volatility clustering and other stylised facts **without
  fitting to historical data** — evidence that microstructure and order flow, not fitted parameters, generate
  the facts. Explicitly positioned for execution-algorithm testing and policy/regime scenarios.
- Limitation: baseline agents are simple; fidelity judged only on summary statistics, not on predictive
  performance.

### arXiv 2311.07738v2 — Revisiting Cont's Stylized Facts for Modern Stock Markets (2023)
- Question: do Cont's (2001) 11 stylised facts still hold, and do they hold for each individual stock?
- Method: intraday 1-minute and trade-time returns of the Dow 30, Oct 2018–Mar 2019, replica of each fact.
- Finding: **8 of 11 supported; 3 not** — gain/loss asymmetry (Fact #3), the leverage effect (Fact #9), and
  asymmetry in timescales (Fact #11) show no consistent signal across individual symbols. Skew ranges from
  negative to positive across stocks; extreme returns are split between gains and losses; volume–volatility
  correlation is strong in clock-time but vanishes in event-time.
- Limitation: one 5-month window, one index universe (Dow 30); index-level facts may still hold even where
  single-name facts do not.

### arXiv 2509.19663v3 — Long-range dependence in financial markets and generative modelling (2026)
- Question: is LRD in mean or volatility, and can generative models reproduce it?
- Method: R/S, DFA, segmented multifractal analysis and ARFIMA–FIGARCH (Student-t) on equities, commodities
  and energy; then Quant GANs evaluated against econometric benchmarks.
- Finding: **limited persistence in mean returns, pronounced long memory in conditional volatility**, with
  a non-negligible change in equity scaling after 2020. GANs reproduce heavy tails and aspects of volatility
  clustering but **do not reproduce the magnitude/persistence of LRD** — synthetic data that understates
  persistence will understate tail risk and drawdowns.
- Limitation: the GAN comparison is one architecture; ARFIMA–FIGARCH is the benchmark rather than a
  ground truth.

## 3. Learnings applicable to this repo

### L1. R/S is the right Hurst estimator under heavy tails — but it carries no error bar
- **Source**: `arXiv 1201.4786v1` (2012) — Hurst exponent estimation under heavy-tailed distributions.
- **Finding**: R/S and GHE are robust to stable-distribution tails (α down to 1.1); MF-DFA and DMA are
  biased. GHE has the lowest bias and variance; Gaussian confidence intervals around 0.5 are invalid for
  financial returns.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py::hurst_exponent`.
- **Status**: `partial` — the module already uses R/S on the *return* series (the paper's recommended
  input) and warns against feeding price levels, so the estimator choice is correct; but it returns a
  clamped point estimate (`h = max(0.0, min(1.0, h))`) with no finite-sample uncertainty.
- **Concrete step**: carry the GPH-style standard error beside H (or add a GHE cross-check) and report H's
  finite-sample band instead of a bare clamped number, so `book_risk.drawdown_envelope`'s `T^(H−1/2)` factor
  inherits an uncertainty.

### L2. Multifractal "width" from short or persistent series is mostly artefact
- **Source**: `arXiv 1307.2014v2` (2013) — multifractal effects generated by monofractal signals;
  `arXiv 2211.00728v2` (2023) — genuine multifractality is due to temporal correlations.
- **Finding**: finite length manufactures a non-zero Δh/Δα even for monofractal series, growing linearly in
  the autocorrelation exponent γ and decaying as a power law in length; genuine multifractality requires
  long-range correlations, fat tails alone cannot create it.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/complexity.py` (no MF-DFA exists; nearest shipped read is
  `permutation_entropy`).
- **Status**: `absent`.
- **Concrete step**: do not add MF-DFA without a shuffle-surrogate null. If a multifractal read is ever
  added, gate the Δα/Δh claim behind a phase-randomised/shuffled surrogate test and refuse the claim below
  the finite-size threshold.

### L3. Long memory lives in volatility, not in returns — target the right series
- **Source**: `arXiv physics/0601174v2` (2006) — DFA long memory and volatility clustering;
  `arXiv 2509.19663v3` (2026) — LRD in financial markets; `arXiv 1410.3394v1` (2014) — volatility is rough.
- **Finding**: returns show no significant long memory; volatility does, and GARCH(1,1) filtering removes
  it, identifying it as volatility clustering. A rough (H≈0.1) volatility process also triggers classical
  "long memory" tests, so the two are statistically confusable.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/long_memory.py::memory_parameter` (GPH + local Whittle) as
  called by `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py::memory_profile`.
- **Status**: `partial` — the module's stated intent is the memory of the *variance* process, but
  `memory_profile` passes the raw `returns` series, so GPH is fitted to returns, whose d is the (near-zero)
  mean memory, not the volatility memory the label implies.
- **Concrete step**: feed `rv = returns**2` (the module already accepts an `rv` argument in `rv_forecast`)
  or `log|returns|` into `memory_parameter` for the variance-memory leg, or relabel the record so a
  near-zero `d` on returns is not read as "no volatility long memory".

### L4. Rough-volatility H≈0.1 is real for log-vol but realized-vol roughness is partly an artefact
- **Source**: `arXiv 1410.3394v1` (2014) — Volatility is rough; `arXiv 2203.13820v3` (2023) — Rough
  volatility: fact or artefact?
- **Finding**: log-volatility behaves as fBm with H≈0.1 and RFSV improves RV forecasts; but realized
  volatility always looks rough (H<0.5) even when spot volatility is Brownian, because estimation error is
  rough. Chasing realized-vol roughness as if it were spot-vol roughness is a classic error.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/volatility_models.py` (GARCH(1,1), EWMA, Yang-Zhang, bipower
  and quarticity proxies — no roughness estimator).
- **Status**: `absent`.
- **Concrete step**: if a roughness read is added, estimate H on log realized variance with the normalized
  p-th variation estimator and attach the Cont–Das caveat that realized-vol roughness overstates spot-vol
  roughness; keep it REPORT-ONLY like the existing proxies.

### L5. A Hawkes branching ratio ≈ 1 is the market's default; endogeneity is measurable from event counts
- **Source**: `arXiv 1302.1405v2` (2013) — critical reflexivity; `arXiv 1201.3572v2` (2012) — quantifying
  reflexivity; `arXiv 1403.5227v3` (2014) — branching ratio from mean and variance of counts.
- **Finding**: the Hawkes kernel integrates to ~1 across 1998–2011 (markets near criticality), and the
  exogenous share of price moves fell from ~70% (1998) to <30% (post-2007) on short windows. The branching
  ratio n can be approximated from only the mean and variance of event counts.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/orderflow.py::vpin` / `knife_guard_vpin` (order-flow
  toxicity; no Hawkes, and no tick/order-arrival series is fetched).
- **Status**: `absent`.
- **Concrete step**: add a cheap self-excitation proxy — branching ratio from the mean/variance of event
  counts within volume buckets — as a diagnostic beside `vpin`. Do not claim a full Hawkes fit without
  event-time data.

### L6. Extreme losses and gains are conditionally asymmetric; tail arrival is self-exciting
- **Source**: `arXiv 2202.01043v2` (2022) — 2T-POT Hawkes asymmetric tail quantiles.
- **Finding**: losses contribute ~2.2× as much to a shared conditional intensity and decay ~4.6× faster
  than gains; the asymmetric Hawkes tail model beats GARCH-EVT for VaR/ES at 5% coverage and below.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/tail_risk.py::tail_risk` (historical VaR/CVaR with a conformal
  band — no arrival-process asymmetry).
- **Status**: `absent` for the Hawkes-arrival leg (the module's own `basis` already admits the producer VaR
  fails the Kupiec test).
- **Concrete step**: do not yet claim a Hawkes tail. Short of a full fit, at minimum split the historical
  VaR/CVaR tail into left and right and record the left/right exceedance clustering (Fano factor of
  threshold exceedances) — the direction of the 2T-POT finding — instead of a symmetric tail.

### L7. A horizon-scaling tail-index estimator (MPMR) needs no confidence level
- **Source**: `arXiv 2209.07092v2` (2022) — Measuring tail risks.
- **Finding**: for Pareto tails, MPMR(n) ∝ n^η and ξ̂ = 1/η̂ is a robust low-bias tail index, computed from
  sub-sampled block maxima (M=600, n up to N(1−1/e)), competitive with Hill under Student-t.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/tail_risk.py::tail_risk` and
  `tradingagents/strategies/book_risk.py::simple_var` / `cvar` (historical quantiles only; no tail index).
- **Status**: `absent`.
- **Concrete step**: add an MPMR tail-index read — the n-scaling slope of log block-maxima — so the tail
  book carries an exponent that can be extrapolated in horizon, rather than only a fixed-α quantile.

### L8. Depth risk does not scale as sqrt(T) — the repo already knows this, in one place
- **Source**: `arXiv 2312.05655v1` (2023) — unbiased adjustment of risk estimators.
- **Finding**: sqrt-time scaling to long horizons and extreme percentiles is biased; a conservative scaling
  calibrated against backtests is preferable.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/book_risk.py::drawdown_envelope` — depth measures are already
  rescaled by `horizon ** (hurst - 0.5)`, with the `T^(H−1/2)` self-similarity argument in the module
  comment (paper 2608.00127).
- **Status**: `shipped` (but REPORT-ONLY — the module comment states the rescaling is deliberately not
  wired into `risk_governor` / `govern` / `drawdown_gate`).
- **Concrete step**: no new scaling law; the open item is validating/deciding whether the `T^(H−1/2)`
  scaling (with an H that carries no uncertainty, see L1) may reach the governor. Keep it report-only until
  H has an error bar.

### L9. Three classic "facts" are not facts at the individual-name intraday level
- **Source**: `arXiv 2311.07738v2` (2023) — Revisiting Cont's stylized facts.
- **Finding**: gain/loss asymmetry, the leverage effect, and asymmetry in timescales were **not** supported
  across Dow-30 single names on intraday data; skew varies by sign, extreme returns split between gains and
  losses, and volume–volatility correlation vanishes in event-time.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/statistical.py::correlation_matrix` and
  `tradingagents/strategies/complexity.py` (no leverage/asymmetry diagnostic), and any analyst narration of
  a "leverage effect".
- **Status**: `absent` (no test; the risk is narrated claims).
- **Concrete step**: if the market/social analysts assert a leverage or gain/loss asymmetry, add a computed
  diagnostic — correlation of r_t with |r_{t+1}|, and the sign split of extreme returns — so the claim is
  backed by a number or suppressed. Index-level claims are unaffected.

### L10. Complexity-entropy measures need an ensemble/multiscale form to be comparable
- **Source**: `arXiv 2507.23414v1` (2025) — MF-DFA and RCMSE complexity of BTC/GBPUSD/gold/gas;
  `arXiv 2504.01974v1` (2025) — complexity-entropy plane efficiency score for crypto.
- **Finding**: combining a complexity measure with entropy (the complexity-entropy plane) or with multiscale
  sample entropy (RCMSE) yields a comparative complexity/inefficiency score; Bitcoin shows the highest
  complexity, attributed to nonlinear correlations.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/complexity.py::permutation_entropy` (single-scale) —
  `permutation_entropy`, `approximate_entropy`, `lz_complexity` only.
- **Status**: `partial` — scalar entropy reads exist but there is no complexity-entropy plane and no
  multiscale entropy.
- **Concrete step**: add a normalized complexity (e.g. LZ) vs permutation-entropy pair as a 2-D
  inefficiency read, and/or a coarse-graining multiscale entropy, so scores are comparable across assets
  and horizons.

### L11. A microstructure ABM can generate stylised facts without fitting — useful as a scenario engine
- **Source**: `arXiv 2312.14903v2` (2023) — scalable agent-based market simulation.
- **Finding**: heterogeneous agents + continuous double auction reproduce fat tails and volatility
  clustering without calibrating to historical data; positioned for execution and regime scenario testing.
- **Repo surface**: `tradingagents/strategies/backtest_engine.py` (order state machine + bar matching — no
  synthetic generator).
- **Status**: `absent`.
- **Concrete step**: an ABM is out of proportion to current needs; if stress scenarios without history are
  ever required, a minimal order-book ABM belongs beside `backtest_engine`, not inside it. Record as a
  deferral, not a task.

## 4. Where this repo is already ahead of the literature

- `tradingagents/strategies/book_risk.py::drawdown_envelope` already applies a Hurst-corrected `T^(H−1/2)`
  depth-scaling under long memory — exactly the anti-sqrt-time correction the risk-scaling literature
  argues for — and pairs it with Lo (2002) Sharpe estimation uncertainty.
- `tradingagents/strategies/tail_risk.py::tail_risk` carries a VaR/CVaR *with its quality and uncertainty*,
  a conformal widening band, and a Kupiec/Christoffersen coverage verdict reported beside the number. The
  econophysics tail literature mostly reports a point tail estimate; the reliability layer is ahead.
- `tradingagents/strategies/long_memory.py` uses semiparametric GPH + local Whittle with an asymptotic
  standard error, refuses rather than fabricating when a window is too short or reaches the as-of date, and
  gates the whole layer off by default — cleaner than the review papers' "compute a Hurst number" practice.
- `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py::hurst_exponent` feeds R/S the return series and explicitly
  warns against price levels; this is the input discipline the heavy-tail literature requires.

## 5. Defects or risks the literature exposes

1. **Variance-memory estimated on the wrong series.** `tradingagents/strategies/long_memory.py::memory_parameter`
   (def at line ~138) is documented as the memory of the *variance* process, but
   `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py::memory_profile` passes the raw `returns` into it, so GPH is
   fitted to returns — whose long memory is ~zero. `physics/0601174v2` and `2509.19663v3` show the long
   memory is in volatility, not returns. As wired, a near-zero `d` will be read as "no long memory" when the
   volatility memory was never measured.
2. **Hurst reported without finite-sample or tail uncertainty.**
   `tradingagents/strategies/mean_reversion.py::hurst_exponent` clamps H to [0,1] and returns a bare float.
   `1201.4786v1` shows heavy tails widen the sampling distribution of every estimator (even the robust R/S),
   and `2203.13820v3` shows realized-vol roughness can be an estimation artefact. The clamped point H then
   propagates into `book_risk.py::drawdown_envelope`'s `T^(H−1/2)` factor (module comment at ~line 1271), so
   a biased H biases the drawdown envelope.
3. **Tail model has no arrival asymmetry.** `tradingagents/strategies/tail_risk.py::tail_risk` (def at line
   174) uses historical VaR/CVaR only. `2202.01043v2` shows extreme-loss and extreme-gain arrivals are
   asymmetrically self-exciting and that conditional EVT beats GARCH-EVT/historical at ≤5% coverage; the
   module's own `basis` already notes the producer VaR fails Kupiec. The tail is therefore known-fragile and
   known-symmetric where the data-generating process is asymmetric.
4. **Narrated stylised facts that do not hold for single names.** Any analyst claim of a leverage effect or
   gain/loss asymmetry on an individual intraday equity is unsupported: `2311.07738v2` finds no consistent
   signal for Facts #3 (gain/loss asymmetry), #9 (leverage) or #11 (timescale asymmetry) across the Dow 30.
   The repo has no diagnostic to refute such a claim (surface:
   `tradingagents/strategies/statistical.py::correlation_matrix`), so it must be computed or suppressed.

## 6. Limits of this review

- Read in full text (abstract → method → results/conclusion): the 15 papers in §2. The remaining ~45
  high-relevance rows were only abstract-swept; no medium/low row was deep-read.
