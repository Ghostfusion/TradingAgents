### Creating quality portfolios using score-based models: a systematic review — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4
- **Status**: retrieved (full text + Tables 1–11 via /tables/N). Bibliometric SLR: Scopus, 1934–2021, 97 articles, English, journal articles only; VOSviewer + Biblioshiny.
- **Taxonomy** (Table 9, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/9): 2 families. DEBT-based, single-scrip, ratio/probabilistic: Z-score (Altman 1968), O-score (Ohlson 1980), M-score (Beneish 1999). EQUITY-based, portfolio: F-score (Piotroski 2000, value stocks = top 20th percentile B/M), G-score (Mohanram 2005, growth stocks = lowest 20th percentile B/M), C-score (Montier 2008/2021, overpriced), L-score (Dorantes 2013, value stocks).
- **Scoring logic**: Z = ratio-based; O = probabilistic (9 factors); M = ratio-based; F/G/C/L = binary 0/1 summed.
- **Exact score definitions** (tables):
  - F = F1+…+F9 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/5): F1=1 if ROA>0; F2=1 if operating cash flow>0; F3=1 if ΔROA>0; F4=1 if (OCF/Total assets)>ROA; F5=1 if leverage decreases; F6=1 if current ratio improves; F7=1 if no new shares issued last year; F8=1 if gross margin improves; F9=1 if asset turnover improves. Decomposition: Profitability (F1–F4) + Leverage/Liquidity (F5–F7) + Efficiency (F8–F9), range 0–9.
  - G = G1+…+G8 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/6): G1=1 if ROA>industry median; G2=1 if CFO>industry median; G3=1 if CFO>net income; G4=1 if variance(ROA)<industry median; G5=1 if variance of YoY sales growth<industry median; G6=1 if R&D intensity>industry median; G7=1 if Capex intensity>industry median; G8=1 if Advertising intensity>industry median. Decomposition: Profitability (G1–G3) + Earnings Variability (G4–G5) + Spending Conservatism (G6–G8), range 0–8.
  - C = C1+…+C6 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/7): C1=1 if divergence between net income and cash flows exceeds prior year; C2=1 if receivable days up; C3=1 if inventory days up; C4=1 if other current assets up; C5=1 if depreciation/gross fixed assets down; C6=1 if total assets grew >10% last year. Range 0–6.
  - L = L1+…+L8 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/8): L1 inventory positive change; L2 accounts receivable positive change; L3 gross margins positive change; L4 S&A expenses positive change; L5 effective tax rate positive change; L6 capital expenditure positive change; L7 margin-to-cost ratio positive change; L8 working capital positive change. Range 0–8.
  - Z-score thresholds: Z(M) ≥3 sound / ≤1.8 high bankruptcy risk; Z(NM) ≥2.6 sound / ≤1.1 high risk; separate Z(EM). 72%+ accuracy over 2 years (Eidelman 1995). O-score default probability = O/(1+O), ~90% accuracy over 2 years. M-score > −2.22 ⇒ possible manipulation, estimated >71%.
- **Bands → interpretation** (Table 9): Z ≥3 good/≤1.8 poor; O low-prob good/high-prob poor; M ≤−2.22 good, ≥−1.78 poor; F 7–9 good, 0–3 poor; G 6–8 good, 0–2 poor; C 0–2 good, 5–6 poor; L 6–8 good, 0–2 poor.
- **Recurring constituent metrics across models** (Table 10, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03888-4/tables/10): Total assets, Current ratio, Sales/Revenue, Earnings, Leverage, External factors, Working capital, Accruals, Cash flows, Sales/Admin expenses, Margins. Most-utilised: total assets, current ratio, sales/revenue, earnings, margins, cash flows, accruals, leverage, working capital, industry/economic factors.
- **Standardisation**: NOT specified. Scores are raw binary counts (F/G/C/L) or ratio formulas (Z/M) / probabilities (O). No z-scoring, percentiles, clipping, winsorising, or sector neutralisation is described anywhere in the article. Limitation stated: scores capture quality not magnitude of change; weighting to capture magnitude suggested as future work.
- **Portfolio sorts / bucket count / weighting**: NOT specified. Only the screening cutoff is given (value = top 20th percentile B/M; growth = bottom 20th percentile B/M). No decile/quintile count, no equal- vs value-weighting rule is reported. GAP.
- **Reported performance evidence**: Z ~72%+ accuracy, 2-yr horizon; O ~90% 2-yr; M > −2.22 flagged manipulation; F portfolio 7–9 significantly outperforms index, low F underperforms and may bankrupt (Agrawal 2015); G 6–8 outperforms (MohanRam 2005); C high 5–6 underperforms within 2 years; Montier C negative portfolio + equal positive index ≈ >8% consistent US return. International replication lists: Z in 31 European countries + US/China/Colombia (Altman et al. 2017), NZ, Kenya, Jordan, Malaysia, Indonesia, India, UAE, Mexico, Pakistan, Korea, Japan, Sri Lanka, Hong Kong, Zimbabwe; O in Canada, Thailand, China, Japan, HK, Indonesia, India, Iran, Europe; M in Malaysia (14/17 fraud firms detected), Greece, Vietnam, Iraq, Zimbabwe, Bosnia, Iran, Ghana, Nigeria, Turkey, Bangladesh, Romania, China, India, Lebanon, Slovakia/Czech. F in Japan, Europe, India, South Africa, Australia, Brazil, Mexico, Thailand, EAFE, Indonesia, Germany (3-yr). G in Eurozone, India, Brazil, Thailand, Taiwan. C in South Africa. L in Mexico, India (Nifty 500).
- **Survival against size/value/profitability/investment controls**: NOT reported. The review contains no factor-adjusted (size/value/profitability/investment) regression evidence; it only notes B/M is used for initial screening and F-score returns persist ~2 years then dilute (Piotroski 2000). GAP.
- **Limitations named in the review**: subjective scoring criteria selected from author bias → inconsistency; quantitative-only (ignores qualitative factors); over-prioritises short-term metrics (profitability, revenue growth, ROI) vs long-term sustainability; overlooks intangibles (brand, customer loyalty, IP); reverse-engineering possible to inflate score; models static/less adaptable to changing conditions; historical-data-driven → poorer predictive power under changed industry dynamics/black swans; industry-specific fit, poor for unconventional/emerging business models; developed decades ago and cannot predict modern times (Boritz 2007; Wu 2010); geographic failures listed for Z/O and M; sensitivity to accounting-practice changes (Z and C affected; F and M resistant — Nadar & Wadhwa 2020); FD scores capture quality not magnitude. NOVEL/relevance erosion: F losing relevance (Novy-Marx 2013); combining G then refining with F diluted results (Novy-Marx 2013); G failed in Brazil (Galdi & Lopes 2013); Hyde 2018 Australia — high F-score did not generate four alphas.
- **Limitations explicitly NOT present** (grep of full article text, 0 matches): look-ahead bias, restatements, sector concentration, small-cap dominance, data-snooping, publication bias, survivorship, winsorising. The only bias handling mentioned: authors avoided self-citation over-emphasis and temporal bias via a long 1934–2021 window.
- **Engine-relevant notes**: Inputs needed = annual/quarterly fundamentals (total assets, current ratio, revenue, earnings, leverage, working capital, accruals, operating cash flow, gross margin, S&A, capex, R&D, advertising, inventory, receivables, depreciation, shares issued). Engine HAS these. F-score is directly implementable from quarterly/annual fundamentals (binary 9-item). G-score needs industry medians + R&D/advertising intensity (advertising often sparse in fundamentals feeds). Z/O/M need bankruptcy/manipulation labels or coefficients not given in this review (Z formula coefficients and O 9-factor coefficients are in tables not transcribed here — they are image/table-only). No IV, no news, no prediction-market input used by any model.

### Investor Sentiment and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns — https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jwurgler/papers/wurgler_baker_cross_section.pdf (Baker & Wurgler, Journal of Finance LXI(4), Aug 2006, pp.1645–1680)
- **Status**: retrieved (full text incl. all equations and Tables I–VIII).
- **Full proxy list with source series + required transformation** (Section III.C, Table II):
  1. **CEFD** = closed-end fund discount = year-end value-weighted average difference between NAV of closed-end stock-fund shares and market prices. Source: Neal & Wheatley (1998) 1962–1993; CDA/Wiesenberger 1994–1998; Wall Street Journal turn-of-year issues 1999–2001. Transformation: none (inversely related to sentiment). Timing: current t.
  2. **TURN** = NYSE share turnover = reported share volume ÷ average shares listed (NYSE Fact Book). Transformation: natural log of raw turnover, detrended by its own past 5-year moving average (to remove exponential positive trend and the May-1975 fixed-commission effect). Timing: lagged 1 year (t−1).
  3. **NIPO** = annual number of IPOs (Jay Ritter website, updating Ibbotson, Sindelar & Ritter 1994). Transformation: none; current t.
  4. **RIPO** = average annual first-day returns on IPOs (same source). Transformation: none; lagged 1 year (t−1).
  5. **S** = equity share in new issues = gross equity issuance ÷ (gross equity + gross long-term debt issuance), Federal Reserve Bulletin. Transformation: none; current t.
  6. **PD−ND** = dividend premium = year-end log ratio of value-weighted average market-to-book ratios of payers vs nonpayers (from Baker & Wurgler 2004). Transformation: none (already log); lagged 1 year (t−1).
- **PCA / orthogonalisation recipe (exact, in order)**: (1) Estimate the first principal component of the SIX proxies AND their lags (12 series) → first-stage index with 12 loadings. (2) Compute correlation between first-stage index and current and lagged values of each proxy. (3) For each proxy select the lead or lag (whichever) has higher correlation with the first-stage index. (4) Define SENTIMENT as the first principal component of the correlation matrix of those SIX selected variables, rescaling coefficients so the index has unit variance. Each index component first STANDARDISED. PC1 explains 49% of sample variance; correlation between 12-term first-stage index and SENTIMENT = 0.95 (little info lost dropping the six other-subscript terms).
- **Exact weights** (Eq. 2): `SENTIMENT_t = −0.241·CEFD_t + 0.242·TURN_{t−1} + 0.253·NIPO_t + 0.257·RIPO_{t−1} + 0.112·S_t − 0.283·PD^{−ND}_{t−1}`. All six expected signs; price/behaviour variables (RIPO, PD−ND, TURN, CEFD) lead firm-supply variables (S, NIPO).
- **Orthogonalised index** (Eq. 3): regress EACH of the six RAW proxies on: growth in industrial production (Fed Statistical Release G.17), growth in consumer durables, nondurables and services (BEA NIA Table 2.10), growth in employment, and an NBER-recession dummy (Table II caption; §III.C text omits 'employment' — discrepancy reported). Residuals labelled ⊥. Then run the identical PCA+lead/lag procedure. `SENTIMENT⊥_t = −0.198·CEFD⊥_t + 0.225·TURN⊥_{t−1} + 0.234·NIPO⊥_t + 0.263·RIPO⊥_{t−1} + 0.211·S⊥_t − 0.243·PD^{−ND,⊥}_{t−1}`. PC1 explains 53% of variance; only the first eigenvalue >1.00. Orthogonalising is second-order (does not qualitatively change components); ⊥ proxies slightly MORE correlated with each other than raw proxies.
- **Scaling**: both indexes standardised to zero mean, unit variance (a one-unit move = one SD).
- **Sub-indices / pre-1964 detail**: The paper defines NO pre-1964/post-1964 split. Closest constructs: main index 1962–2001 (six proxies, annual); reduced long index 1935–2001 = first PC of CEFD, S, and lagged TURN, orthogonalised to consumption growth and NBER recessions (industrial production unavailable pre-full-period). The widely-cited monthly series (post-2006 updates, Wurgler's site, starting 1965:07) is outside this paper — mark the '1964 sub-index' premise UNVERIFIED for this source.
- **Aggregation / window / update lag**: indexes measured ANNUALLY, 1962–2001 (Table II title says 1962–2000). Monthly returns 1963–2001; accounting data for fiscal years ending in calendar t−1 matched to monthly returns Jul t → Jun t+1 (Fama-French 1992 convention). SENTIMENT_{t−1} (prior year-end value) forecasts monthly returns Jan–Dec of year t (Eqs. 4–5). So the index for year t is only knowable after year-end t; effective operational lag ≈ 1 calendar year for annual use (no higher-frequency construction in this paper).
- **Sort methodology**: equal-weighted decile portfolios using NYSE breakpoints (deciles defined on NYSE firms). Returns binned by characteristic decile × sign of prior-year SENTIMENT⊥; report averages in positive vs negative months and their difference. Value-weighting deliberately avoided (theory: large firms less affected).
- **Predictive regressions** (Eqs. 4–5): `RX_{i=High,t} − RX_{i=Low,t} = c + d·SENTIMENT_{t−1} + u` and with controls RMRF, SMB, HML, UMD (SMB/HML excluded when they are the dependent variable). Bootstrapped SEs (Stambaugh 1999 bias correction). Coefficients are % per month; one unit = one SD.
- **Reported results — level coefficients (d ×100, %/mo)** Table V, SENTIMENT⊥ w/ controls / (univariate): SMB(ME) −0.3 (p .15)/−0.4(.06); Age High−Low +0.2(.04)/+0.5(.01); σ High−Low −0.4(.01)/−0.9(.00); E>0−E<0 +0.4(.02)/+0.8(.00); D>0−D=0 +0.4(.00)/+0.8(.00); PPE/A +0.1(.53)/+0.4(.07); RD/A +0.1(.62)/−0.3(.24); HML +0.1(.68)/+0.2(.25); EF/A High−Low −0.1(.40)/−0.2(.13); GS High−Low −0.1(.51)/−0.1(.23); BE/ME Med−Low +0.1(.35)/+0.3(.06); EF/A High−Med −0.2(.00)/−0.4(.00); GS High−Med −0.2(.00)/−0.4(.00); BE/ME High−Med −0.1(.53)/−0.1(.30); EF/A Med−Low +0.2(.00)/+0.2(.00); GS Med−Low +0.2(.07)/+0.3(.01). SENTIMENT and SENTIMENT⊥ coefficients near-identical.
- **Sort magnitudes** (Table III): when SENTIMENT⊥<0, bottom-ME decile 2.37%/mo vs top 0.92%; top-decile Age 0.54%/mo LESS when pessimistic but 0.85%/mo MORE when optimistic; high-σ earn 2.41%/mo when sentiment low; profitable−unprofitable +0.61%/mo when sentiment positive but −0.95%/mo when negative; payers−nonpayers +0.75% vs −0.89%. U-shaped conditional difference for GS/EF/BE-ME (extremes move together vs middle).
- **Most-affected stocks**: young, small, high-volatility, unprofitable, non-dividend-paying, extreme-growth, distressed — the hard-to-value/hard-to-arbitrage region. Horizon: monthly returns over the following year (Jan–Dec t).
- **Robustness / validation**: RMRF/SMB/HML/UMD controls attenuate but most effects stay significant; post-1982 dummy no change; removing Jan+Dec slightly stronger; 1935–2001 (Table VI) and out-of-sample 1935–1961 similar except Age (insignificant); out-of-sample RMSE reduction mostly positive in-sample-predictable except Age and post-1980 SMB; conditional market betas (Table VII) typically wrong sign; consumption-growth-beta same; earnings-announcement test 1973–2001 (Table VIII): 12 of 104 coefficients significant at 5%, most negative, congruent with sorts. Stated limitation: announcement test is low-power and only a lower bound. Joint-hypothesis (systematic-risk) caveat acknowledged.
- **Engine-relevant notes**: ALL SIX BW proxies are MARKET/MACRO-wide series, not per-ticker: CEFD (closed-end fund NAV discount — macro-only), TURN (NYSE aggregate turnover — macro-only), NIPO (market IPO count — macro-only), RIPO (mean IPO first-day return — macro-only), S (aggregate equity share of issuance — macro-only), PD−ND (aggregate payer/nonpayer M/B spread — macro-only). The engine's OHLCV + quarterly fundamentals + options IV + news + analyst + prediction markets + FRED inputs cover: (i) FRED gives macro series to replicate the orthogonalisation regressors (industrial production/G.17, consumption, employment, recession flag) but NOT the six raw sentiment proxies; (ii) a market-wide TURN-like proxy IS derivable from the engine's broad US equity basket (aggregate share volume / shares); (iii) per-ticker inputs can only build the CROSS-SECTIONAL conditioning targets (size, age/listing history, volatility from OHLCV, profitability from fundamentals, dividend dummy, B/M, sales growth, distress), not the sentiment index itself. For the covered-call ETF QQQI: high distributions ⇒ dividend payer; option-implied vol from its options chain maps to the high-volatility/hard-to-arbitrage bucket. News relevance scores, analyst ratings/price targets, prediction-market probabilities and IV have NO counterpart in BW and would be additive only.
